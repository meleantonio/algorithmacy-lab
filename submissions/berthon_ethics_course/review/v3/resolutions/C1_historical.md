## P1-020
- **row**: l. 15, "When Alabama's constitutional convention met in 1901 to write a literacy test into the franchise".
- **resolution**: supported. The convention adopted the test.
- **evidence**: *Constitution of the State of Alabama, as adopted by the Constitutional Convention, September 3rd, 1901* (archive.org `constitutionsta00alabgoog`), art. VIII, §181, pp. 83–84: "After the first day of January, nineteen hundred and three, the following persons, and no others … shall be qualified to register as electors …: First—Those who can read and write any article of the Constitution of the United States in the English language, and who are physically unable to work; and those who can read and write any article … and who have worked or been regularly engaged in some lawful employment … for the greater part of the twelve months next preceding …; or. Second—The owner in good faith … of forty acres of land … or … real estate … assessed for taxation at the value of three hundred dollars or more". `D/al1901const_constitutionsta00alabgoog.txt:2172-2207`.
- **proposed v3 text**: no change needed. If the author wants the sentence to carry the adoption, add {+(Constitution of Alabama, 1901, §181)+} after "franchise".
- **note**: §181 offered a property alternative (forty acres or $300) to the read-and-write test, and §180 let veterans, their descendants and "persons who are of good character and who understand the duties and obligations of citizenship" register until 20 December 1902. "A literacy test" is accurate. It was not the only route onto the rolls.
