## Where v4 departs from your framing, and why (decide each)

1. **"Satya assumes disclosure is sufficient."** The essay doesn't say this. It calls chain-of-thought
   transparency "not sufficient or dependable" (para. 9), and most of its principles are controls (paras. 14, 17,
   19). v4 makes a narrower charge: the essay's ethical design *toward people* stops at legibility. Every safeguard
   for people is a text, and the people acted on appear once, at para. 20. If you want the stronger charge back, it
   needs a source.
2. **Who "the user" is.** The essay's "we" is the deploying enterprise. v4 treats "the user" as two people: the staff
   who must read the "human readable evidence" (para. 15), and the people the systems act on. The enterprise
   objection is answered in §5.5.
3. **The history did not treat disclosure as sufficient at first; it treated the signature as sufficient.**
   *L'Estrange* (1934) bound a buyer whether or not the form was read to her. Reading aloud, interpreters'
   certificates and translation duties came later, as reforms. So v4 says the essay stands at the *second* of four
   stages (§3.5): signature, disclosure, check-and-shift-risk, capacity. This keeps your parallel, but its form changes.
4. **Scaffolding is not new as a duty.** *BEQ*'s 2023 special issue (Scherer et al.) and Vredenburgh (2022) already ask
   firms to inform, train or fund experts. v4 claims the historical case and the location (inside the system),
   not the duty itself.
5. **Scaffolding as responsibilization.** This is the strongest objection (Willis 2008; Docherty & Valderrama
   Barragán 2026; Martin 2019). v4 answers it with conditions: the scaffold is built into the system, owed rather
   than offered, fades, and is paired with the operator's check and risk-bearing (§5.5).
6. **Berthon.** v4 cites Berthon, Pitt and Campbell (2019): "information is necessary, but not sufficient", and
   notes that they put education under "informing". v4 separates education from disclosure, which departs from
   their framework. You may want to say so to him directly.
7. **Oral vs literate minds.** v4 makes no cognitive claim (§2.4). Ong's book is about "mentality"; v4 uses Ong only
   for "secondary orality" and leans on Finnegan, Street and Goody's later disavowal.
8. **The orality objection.** Chat interfaces talk, so is AI a *return* to orality? v4 answers in §4.3: the
   governance is written (Ong p. 133; Heyd; Arató & Balázs).
