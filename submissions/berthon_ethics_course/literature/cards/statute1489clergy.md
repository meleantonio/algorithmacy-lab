## Summary
A short chapter of Henry VII's fourth Parliament. Its preamble says that lettered persons, trusting in the Church's privilege, had grown "the more bold" to commit murder, rape, robbery and theft, because they were admitted to benefit of clergy as often as they offended. It enacts that a person "not being within orders" who has once been admitted to clergy shall not be admitted again, and that each such person shall be marked in open court by the gaoler before delivery to the Ordinary: "M" on the brawn of the left thumb for murder, "T" for other felony. A proviso gives a man claiming clergy a second time on the ground that he is in orders a day to produce his letters of orders or a certificate from his Ordinary; if he fails, he loses clergy like a layman. The statute presupposes the reading test (it speaks of "psones lettred") but does not create it.

## Key facts it relies on
- Title (p. 538): "An Acte to take awaye the benefytt of Clergye from certayne psons."
- Preamble (p. 538): "WHERE AS upon trust of privilege of the Churche div[er]s psones lettred hath ben the more bold to cõmitte murdre rape robbery thefte and all othre myschevous dedys, bicause they have ben continuelly admitted to the beneffice of the Clergie as ofte as they did offend".
- Enacting clause (p. 538): "ey psone not being within orders, whiche onys hath ben admytted to the benefice of his Clergie, eftsonys arayned of eny suche offence, be not admitted to have the benefice or privilege of his Clergie".
- Branding (p. 538): "to be marked with a M. upon the brawne of the lefte thumbe, and if he be for eny othre felony, the same psone to be marked with a T. in the same place of the thumbe, and theis markes to be made by the Gaillour openly in the Courte before the Jugge".
- Proviso (p. 538): a clerk in orders without "his tres of his orders or a Ctificat of his Ordinary" is given a day to bring them, failing which he loses clergy "as he shall doo that is without orders".

## Critical notes from the literature
- Facet V2 (Narrows 2): cite this statute for branding and the one-use rule, not for the reading test, which came from court practice after the 1350 ordinance (Cross 1917 p. 552; Gabel 1928, snippets).
- The date: the volume's 1488-9 against Cross's 1487; give "4 Hen. VII c. 13 (1489)" with the volume's heading as the authority.
