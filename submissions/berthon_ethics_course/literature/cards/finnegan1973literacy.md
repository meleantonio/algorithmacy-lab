## Summary
The essay that named the "great divide" as a target. Finnegan opens on a policy statement: René Maheu, UNESCO's director-general, told the 1965 Teheran World Congress of Ministers of Education on the Eradication of Illiteracy that mankind "can be divided into two main groups", the difference between them attributable (the snippet cuts off) to literacy. She then asks whether "non-literate" must mean "without literature", and whether individuals in non-literate or largely non-literate cultures are "ipso facto less creative, thoughtful, self-aware or individually" (cut off) than literate ones. The search ran over the whole volume, so I use only snippets that carry this chapter's running head or its notes. Goody (1987, *The Interface between the Written and the Oral*) later cites this essay as the source of the charge that his account was "a Great Divide theory".
