## 3. Ranked shortlist, tier A only (ABDC A or A*)

My ranking is a judgment on fit evidence that is thin: publisher author-guideline pages for Elsevier, Wiley, SAGE, T&F, Emerald, INFORMS and MISQ returned HTTP 403, so article types and word limits are unread for most journals. Treat it as a reading order, not a verdict. The author should read each journal's live guidelines before choosing.

**1. Information and Organization (ABDC A*).**
*For.* Scope (from aggregator copies, secondary) is the relationship between information technologies and social organization, with a stated interest in qualitative and critical work, "critical theory and science and technology studies", and "not restricted to any specific theoretical or disciplinary position." It has published exactly the neighbouring conversation: Cecez-Kecmanovic (2025) on ethics of automated algorithmic decision-making from a posthumanist perspective (10.1016/j.infoandorg.2025.100587), Arora et al. (2023) on data colonialism (10.1016/j.infoandorg.2023.100478), Scott and Orlikowski (2025) on sociomaterial genealogies of AI (10.1016/j.infoandorg.2025.100558, the closest "arguing from history" in the IS pool), and Kellogg et al. (2025). The author's wider programme is organization theory, which this journal reads.
*Against.* I read no author guidelines (Elsevier blocked), so I do not know whether it takes a no-data conceptual paper under any article type. An editor would likely ask for an IS or organizational anchor and for engagement with sociomaterial theory; a legal-and-moral history of literacy may read as outside IS. No IS journal returned any article arguing from the history of literacy.

**2. Marketing Theory (ABDC A).**
*For.* The only journal in the pool whose guidelines (a Sage-hosted mirror, not the journal's domain) name the form: "theory papers and speculative essays, review articles", capped at 10,000 words including references, with 2,000-3,000-word think pieces. It carries platform and algorithm work (Wei and Geiger 2024, 10.1177/14705931241275558; Pellandini-Simányi 2023, 10.1177/14705931231160828; Bajde et al. 2023, 10.1177/14705931231202359) and sits near critical consumer scholarship in the vein of Giesler and Veresiu. The paper was written for a marketing-and-technology scholar.
*Against.* The searcher found no article arguing from history, and none of the paper's cited works. An editor would want a contribution to marketing theory, not organization or design theory, and the claimed "contributions to organizational and design theory" would have to be reframed. Sage AI policy requires disclosure of AI that affects methodology, analysis, results or conclusions.

**3. Journal of Public Policy & Marketing (ABDC A).**
*For.* Berthon, Pitt and Campbell (2019), "Addictive De-Vices: A Public Policy Analysis of Sources and Solutions to Digital Addiction", is in this journal (JPP&M 38(4): 451-468, 10.1177/0743915619859852, Crossref-verified). The journal has a recent literacy-construct paper (Papaoikonomou et al. 2023, "Ethical Consumer Literacy", 10.1177/07439156231202746) and AI-policy commentary (Du and Sen 2023, 10.1177/07439156231186573). The paper's three design obligations are policy-shaped.
*Against.* Two sources disagree on the conceptual article type: the AMA page (https://www.ama.org/submission-guidelines, read 2026-10-13) lists "Bridging Theory and Practice (conceptual)" at about 4,000 words, while the Sage mirror lists only Research Article, Special Issue Article and Policy Note. **Every submission needs a Policy Contribution Statement of at most 300 words** on page 1 stating the policy conversation, how it extends the marketing-and-policy literature and which stakeholders are affected. A 4,000-word conceptual slot would force a large cut of a paper that now runs longer. No historical-analogy article found. The AMA AI rule says AI-generated content "must be clearly identified in the main document."

**Close fourths (also tier A).** *Journal of Information Technology* (A*): short argumentative pieces on algorithms and platforms (Riemer and Peter 2021, 10.1177/02683962211013358; Currie et al. 2025, 10.1177/02683962251378815). *JAIS* (A*): Research Perspectives (about 10,000 words, "discussion pieces that question the field's assumptions and institutions") and Theory categories are the exact slot, and Gal, Hansen and Lee (2022, 10.17705/1jais.00784) is a no-data ethical-analysis precedent; but at least one author must be an AIS member and the paper would need IS framing. *Science, Technology, & Human Values* (A): Cellard (2024) on transparency theatre (10.1177/01622439241294008) is close; needs STS framing.

**Not realistic.** MIS Quarterly and Information Systems Research. I found no research-commentary or essay category in ISR; MISQ's "Issues and Opinions" (30 pages, "fully developed position statements", per a search summary I could not confirm) is a forward-looking IS position slot, not a historical ethics argument. Journal of Consumer Research has a real conceptual-paper channel (https://consumerresearcher.com/conceptual-papers, read 2026-10-13, not confirmed to be the official site) but it requires a theory of consumers. JAMS needs a substantive marketing issue.

## 5. Generative-AI policies: what bears on this paper

The author's research was AI-assisted and must be disclosed. Publisher-wide policies read on 2026-10-13:

- **Elsevier** (Information and Organization, JSIS, JBR, Business Horizons, TFSC, Technology in Society, Design Studies): separate AI declaration; "these tools must never be used as a substitute for human critical thinking, expertise and evaluation"; AI is not an author. https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/generative-ai-policies-for-journals
- **SAGE** (JIT, JPP&M, Macromarketing, Marketing Theory, Big Data & Society, New Media & Society, STHV, Organization): disclose AI that generates content "upon submission", "within the methods or acknowledgements"; language-only tools need none; undisclosed inappropriate use can lead to rejection. https://www.sagepub.com/journals/publication-ethics-policies/artificial-intelligence-policy
- **Wiley** (ISJ, Regulation & Governance, NTWE, Journal of Law and Society): "Authors must also disclose their use of AI Technologies when submitting a manuscript"; spelling and grammar excluded. https://authors.wiley.com/ethics-guidelines/index.html
- **Taylor & Francis** (EJIS, ICS, The Information Society): **two forks read two different T&F pages and they differ in strictness.** https://taylorandfrancis.com/our-policies/ai-policy/ says authors "must clearly acknowledge within the article or book any use of Generative AI tools", naming tool and version. The T&F author-services page (https://authorservices.taylorandfrancis.com/editorial-policies/using-ai-in-your-research-and-manuscript-preparations/) reportedly says T&F "does not allow generative AI to create a first draft of a manuscript or sections of a manuscript" and that AI cannot "create or conclude the argument of an article". **If the stricter page governs, T&F journals conflict with an AI-assisted paper. The author should read both pages against how the paper was actually produced.**
- **Emerald** (Information Technology & People, Internet Research): official page blocked. A search summary (secondary, unverified) says drafting text with generative AI is not allowed. Treat as a risk.
- **AMA** (JPP&M): AI-generated content "must be clearly identified in the main document" and noted in the Acknowledgments.
- **JAIS** (its own page, https://aisel.aisnet.org/jais/authorinfo.html): a JAIS Generative AI transparency document must accompany each new and revised submission.
- **Springer Nature** (Ethics and IT, P&T, AI & Society, JAMS) and **MIT Press** (Design Issues), **INFORMS** (ISR), **MISQ**, **OUP** (JCR): policy **not read** from an official page.
