**Agent-drafted. The author has not reviewed it.**

Pool: business ethics, management, organization, management learning and history, platform-work sociology.
Researched 2026-10-13 from anonymous web requests only. No e-mail address, Chrome tool, git or paywall workaround was used. Where a page was behind a bot wall I say so and name what I used instead. Crossref (`api.crossref.org`) was queried without any contact header and supplies every DOI below. Crossref returns the publication year of the record, which for some articles is the online-first year, so a volume year can differ by one. I list family names only; check given names before citing.

Fit judgments rest on titles and the abstract excerpts Crossref returned, not on reading the articles. Treat "how close" as a screening call.

### 2.1 Organization Theory (SAGE; EGOS)

1. **Rankings.** ABDC 2025: A (read). It is not in the 2022 list, so the A rating is new. AJG 2024: not found in the text `journalranking.org` returned (that text was truncated, so this is "not found", not "unrated"). FT50: no. SAGE page shows Impact Factor 7.0.
2. **Scope and article types.** Source: official SAGE author instructions, `journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/ott`, read as SAGE's PDF print dated 27 Aug 2026; checked 2026-10-13. Quote: "Organization Theory (OT) publishes work in all areas of organization theory, including papers that develop new substantive theories or offer theoretical extensions, papers on the history of organization and management theory, meta-theoretical inquiries into the core underpinnings of existing theories, critiques of theories and theoretical developments, and theory-driven reviews of important areas of research." And: "Meaningful new implications or insights for theory must be present in all work published in the journal, regardless of whether such implications or insights are derived from the development of novel ideas into new theory, from a critique of received theory, or from a conceptual synthesis of recent advances." Three types: theory article, review article, and "shorter, essay-style contributions to a debate or conversation" (Controversies and Conversations, which starts from an informal proposal to the editors). The theory-article list of subject areas includes "ethics". I found **no word or page limit** in the guidelines ("papers ... can take a variety of forms and lengths"). Abstract 300 words. APA style. Conceptual papers with no data are the journal's core business.
3. **Fit evidence (Crossref-verified).**
   - Stark, Vanden Broeck (2024). Principles of Algorithmic Management. *Organization Theory* 5(2). doi:10.1177/26317877241257213. Close in topic; Stark is the second author of Rosenblat and Stark, which this paper cites.
   - Faraj, Pachidi (2021). Beyond Uberization: The co-constitution of technology and organizing. *Organization Theory* 2(1). doi:10.1177/2631787721995205. Conceptual, platform work.
   - Glaser, Pollock, D'Adderio (2021). The Biography of an Algorithm: Performing algorithmic technologies in organizations. *Organization Theory* 2(2). doi:10.1177/26317877211004609. Conceptual, algorithms.
   - Hinds, von Krogh (2024). Generative AI, Emerging Technology, and Organizing: Towards a theory of progressive encapsulation. *Organization Theory* 5(4). doi:10.1177/26317877241293478. Essay on AI.
   - Jaser, Tourish (2024). Hegemonic Surveillance at Work: Fabricating the cyberized, totalized and thespianized employee. *Organization Theory* 5(1). doi:10.1177/26317877241235940. Critical, technology and control.
   - History-as-argument in OT: Brennecke, Carnabuci, Ertug (2025), Relational History, doi:10.1177/26317877251379502, is about network research, not analogy. I found no OT article that argues from a historical analogy to a present technology. No article on literacy turned up in any of my queries across the pool.
4. **Conversation fit.** I read the reference lists (as deposited at Crossref) of Stark and Vanden Broeck (140 references) and Hinds and von Krogh (37): no hit for Kellogg, Rosenblat, Edwards, Veale, Giesler, Veresiu, Berthon, Muldoon, Raekstad, Brandt, Kamperman, Graff or Pettit. Deposits can be incomplete, so this is weak evidence. By topic, OT is the place where the algorithmic-management conversation is already running in theory form.
5. **Open calls.** One themed section (configurational organizational phenomena) closes 31 Jan 2027 (search extraction from an AOM-hosted post; not read in full). That belongs to the author's other OT project, not this paper. I found no call on AI, platforms or history.
6. **Practicalities.** Double anonymized. Open access: "The Article Processing Charge (APC) is $1000 USD", with discounts "up to 100%" depending on the lead author's affiliation; EGOS "might be able to offer financial support" (SAGE page above). Preprints accepted ("Accepts preprints? Yes"). Time to first decision and acceptance rate: not found. AI policy: the page defers to Sage's policy (see 3.1). EGOS page (`egos.org/organization_theory/manuscript-submission`) says nothing on AI.
7. **Desk-reject risk.** The paper's contribution is a moral claim about who holds responsibility, with a design-ethics ending. OT requires "implications or insights for theory" about organizations; an editor can read this as applied ethics with a literacy history attached.

**Note on a conflict inside the author's portfolio.** The repository memory records a separate Organization Theory submission (the Lima paper rebuild and the configurational special section). Two papers from one doctoral student to the same small editorial team is a decision for the author. I did not check OT's policy on concurrent submissions.

### 2.2 Business Ethics Quarterly (Cambridge University Press, for the Society for Business Ethics)

1. **Rankings.** ABDC 2025: A. AJG 2024: 3 (secondary); the journal's own metrics page still reports "4" from the 2021 guide and ABDC "A, 2025". FT50: no.
2. **Scope and types.** Source: `cambridge.org/core/journals/business-ethics-quarterly/information/author-instructions/preparing-your-materials` and `.../instructions-for-authors-submission-guidelines`, read 2026-10-13. Quote: "BEQ publishes original research on topics relevant to the ethics of business" and "The journal is open to new ideas, new issues, and new approaches". Research articles come in five kinds: normative theory, descriptive theory, qualitative, quantitative and **historical work**; the last three "must also make an original theoretical contribution", defined as "a significant conceptual advance". Limit: "The length of an initial submission may not exceed 12,000 words" (body and footnotes; excludes abstract, tables, figures, references); total manuscript 50 pages; abstract 150 words. Commentaries "normally limited to 6,000 words". The submission page warns that papers that do not "promise to make a novel, theoretical contribution" are commonly desk-rejected.
3. **Fit evidence.**
   - García-Ruiz, Rocchi (2025). Can Work Be Meaningful Under Algorithmic Management? A MacIntyrean Perspective. *BEQ* 36(1). doi:10.1017/beq.2025.5. Close: normative, algorithmic management; its references include Rosenblat.
   - Harracá, Castelló, Gawer (2023). How Digital Platforms Organize Immaturity: A Sociosymbolic Framework of Platform Power. *BEQ* 33(3). doi:10.1017/beq.2022.40. Close: platform power, conceptual.
   - Jonker (2024). Is Data Labor? Two Conceptions of Work and the User-Platform Relationship. *BEQ* 35(2). doi:10.1017/beq.2024.25. Conceptual analysis of platforms.
   - Kim, Routledge (2021). Why a Right to an Explanation of Algorithmic Decision-Making Should Exist: A Trust-Based Approach. *BEQ* 32(1). doi:10.1017/beq.2021.3. Normative argument on algorithms (so transparency is argued about here).
   - Buhmann, Fieseler (2022). Deep Learning Meets Deep Democracy: Deliberative Governance and Responsible Innovation in Artificial Intelligence. *BEQ* 33(1). doi:10.1017/beq.2021.42. Normative, AI.
   - **Historical analogy: none found.** My Crossref queries on history, analogy, genealogy and literacy returned no research article in 2021-2026 that argues from a historical analogy to technology. "Historical work" is a listed type but it needs primary work plus a theoretical contribution. This is evidence against.
4. **Conversation fit.** Of three BEQ articles whose reference lists I checked, one cites Rosenblat (García-Ruiz and Rocchi); Jonker and Harracá et al. show none of the named authors. BEQ reviews Pettit-adjacent books (e.g. a review of Hussain, *Living with the Invisible Hand*, 2025) but I found no research article with a republican non-domination frame. Weak evidence of an audience for that frame.
5. **Open calls.** Special issue "Business Ethics, Illiberalism, and the New Democratic Order in the Digital Age" (guest editors Scherer, Bloom, Neesham, Li). **Pre-submission workshop application deadline 30 October 2026; workshop 26-27 November 2026 (tentative); submission window 1 March to 30 April 2027; publication estimated 2029.** Sources: `sbeonline.org/2026/04/beq-special-issue-on-business-ethics-illiberalism-and-the-new-democratic-order-in-the-digital-age/` and the AOM CMS post, both read 2026-10-13. The posts give the title only; the call PDF is linked but I did not read it, so I cannot say whether algorithmic intermediaries or history fit its themes.
6. **Practicalities.** Review: manuscripts anonymized; desk decision aimed "within four to six weeks"; full review decision "within three to four months". Acceptance rate: not found. APC/open access: not read (CUP "Fees and pricing" pages not fetched). Preprints: the page says a preprint "shall not be viewed as prior or duplicate publication", while the submission page says submitted papers "may not be posted online during the review process". The two statements conflict; ask the editorial office. **AI policy (quote):** "We do not consider artificial intelligence (AI) tools to meet the accountability requirements of authorship." "You should always declare and describe your use of an AI tool if you have used it to generate text or images." Minimal, non-generative uses (spelling, language checks) need no declaration.
7. **Desk-reject risk.** "No novel theoretical contribution": a reconstruction from history that ends on three design obligations reads to a philosophy-trained editor as application, not conceptual advance, unless the argument against transparency and "AI literacy" is sharpened into a thesis with named opponents.

**Flag.** The editorial board page lists Jeffrey Moriarty (co-editor-in-chief) at Bentley University. If the author is at Bentley, ask the editorial office how it handles in-house submissions before submitting.

If use goes beyond copy-editing, a dedicated section before the references must list the tools. **This is the most demanding disclosure rule in the pool for AI-assisted research.** Time to decision, acceptance rate: not found.

### 2.5 Academy of Management Review (AOM)

1. **Rankings.** ABDC A*. AJG 2024: 4* (secondary). FT50: **yes** (item 3 of the 2026 list as reproduced).
2. **Scope.** Source: `aom.org/.../author-resources/submitting-to-review/`, read 2026-10-13. AMR "seeks novel, insightful and carefully crafted conceptual articles" and is "a theory development journal". Length: roughly 30 double-spaced pages excluding references, tables and figures; "contribution-to-length ratio". Articles "must explicitly and persuasively present the research implications of their work"; propositions not required. Page does not mention historical papers.
3. **Fit evidence.**
   - Roulet, Bothello (2023). An Event-System Perspective on Disruption: Theorizing the Pandemic and Other Discontinuities Through Historical and Fictional Accounts of the Plague. *AMR* 48(4). doi:10.5465/amr.2021.0206. Closest precedent: conceptual theory built from historical accounts without archival work of the authors' own (judged from the title; I did not read it).
   - Lindebaum, Balasubramanian, Ashraf, Haack (2026). A Process Model of Managerial Phronesis in the Age of Generative AI. *AMR*. doi:10.5465/amr.2024.0582. Ethics and AI; its references include Veale.
   - Chen, Zhou, Chan (2026). Algorithm Envelopment in Platform Markets. *AMR* 51(1). doi:10.5465/amr.2023.0156.
   - Raisch, Krakowski (2021). Artificial Intelligence and Management: The Automation-Augmentation Paradox. *AMR* 46(1). doi:10.5465/amr.2018.0072.
   - Moser, Glaser, Lindebaum (2024). Taking Situatedness Seriously in Theorizing About Competitive Advantage Through Artificial Intelligence: A Response to Kemp's "Competitive Advantages Through Artificial Intelligence". *AMR* 49(3). Critical response form.
4. **Conversation fit.** One of three AMR papers checked cites a named author (Veale). Insufficient.
5. **Open calls.** None sought.
6. **Practicalities.** Preprints: "should be taken down during the review process." AI policy (AOM, `aom.org/publications/journals/publishing-with-aom/aom-artificial-intelligence-policy/`, read 2026-10-13, undated): authors "must identify whether AI tools were used" at each stage (conceptualization, research design, data preparation and analysis, presentation of results, writing and editing), name "which model was used and for what purpose", and "explicitly confirm that they carefully reviewed, verified, and accepted any AI-involved output"; disclosure goes in the cover letter and acknowledgements. Acceptance rate: one non-official guide says 5-8%; **not verified**.
7. **Desk-reject risk.** AMR wants theory (constructs, mechanisms, boundary conditions); this paper is a moral and historical argument. Realistic outcome for a first-time doctoral submission is a desk rejection unless the paper is recast as a theory of who holds coordination power in a literacy transition.

### 2.7 Organization Studies (SAGE/EGOS)

## 4. Ranked shortlist for this pool

Ranking is about realistic odds for a single-authored doctoral conceptual paper, not prestige. I excluded journals that cannot take the paper (Organization Studies, AMP, Business History Review) or fall below tier A (M&OH, BEER).

**1. Organization Theory.**
*For.* It is the only journal in the pool whose guidelines name this kind of paper: no data required, essays and "controversies and conversations" accepted, "history of organization and management theory" in scope, "ethics" in the subject list. It has run the closest algorithmic-management work (Stark and Vanden Broeck 2024; Glaser et al. 2021; Faraj and Pachidi 2021), and it matches the author's organization-theory programme and the outline's claim of "contributions to organizational and design theory". ABDC A (new in 2025), open access with a $1,000 APC that may be waived or discounted, preprints allowed.
*Against.* The paper's centre is a moral argument, and OT wants a contribution to theory of organizations; the republican frame and the three design obligations may read as applied ethics. AJG 2024 rating not found, FT50 no. The author has another OT project under way, so two papers to the same editors needs a decision. No word limit in the guidelines, so length must be negotiated through the editors. Time to decision and acceptance rate not found.

**2. Business Ethics Quarterly.**
*For.* The moral frame (internal critique, non-domination) is philosophical, which is BEQ's home. 12,000 words, "historical work" and "normative theory" are listed types, ABDC A, and a special issue on the digital age opens a submission window (1 Mar-30 Apr 2027) with a workshop application due 30 Oct 2026. BEQ publishes the closest normative work on algorithmic management and platform power (García-Ruiz and Rocchi 2025; Harracá et al. 2023). Clear AI rule and stated decision times (4-6 weeks to a desk decision).
*Against.* I found no BEQ article that argues from historical analogy, and BEQ expects a "significant conceptual advance" from its history papers. The editor-in-chief is at Bentley, which creates a possible conflict if the author is also there. AJG is 3 (down from 4). The special issue's themes are unread. The preprint statements conflict. Acceptance rate not found.

**3. Journal of Business Ethics.**
*For.* It has a dedicated Technology and Business Ethics section (section editors Acosta Navas, Bhargava and Diamantis per a search extraction of the board page, needing confirmation), has published analogy-based ethics-of-AI work (Seele and Schultz 2022) and competence-and-AI work (Syed 2026), and takes 8,000-12,000 words. ABDC A and the largest outlet for this literature.
*Against.* It was removed from the FT50 in April 2026, AJG 3. I could not open the journal's own pages (bot wall), so its scope text, review model, decision times and preprint rules are unverified. At its volume a first-page novelty claim is essential.

**Runners-up.** Business & Society (A, AJG 3; best thematic fit but the AI call closed 13 days ago; no preprints; heaviest AI disclosure). Academy of Management Review (A*, FT50; has precedent for theory from historical accounts and for AI ethics; a doctoral-student, no-theory-spine submission is a long shot and its AI policy is also demanding). Organization (A, AJG 3; Connexions essay at 6,000 words fits the form, the register is more political than this paper).
