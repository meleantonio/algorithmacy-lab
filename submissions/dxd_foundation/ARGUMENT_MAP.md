**Conclusion.** Word separation is a historical case of a human-factors principle: re-represent a task
so that an artefact carries work the person would otherwise carry (P1–P6). Coordination through an
algorithmic third party lacks conventions of that kind (P7). The evidence so far favours rules in the
mediator that remove a strategic task and show that it is gone (P8) over aids that add to what the
person must read (P9), and such rules must be held where the mediator's owner cannot withdraw them alone
(P10). Nadella's insider post shares the premise that the model cannot be read and the remedy of
external controls; it assigns authority to the deployer, leaves its residual human tasks where people
perform worst, and is silent on the people the model acts upon (P11, P12). Decision Experience Design is
the discipline that looks for the missing conventions.

## What the argument no longer claims

| Dropped | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Spacing "ignited literacy" | No historian of literacy credits it; Saenger's book "is not a general history of literacy" (p. x); unspaced scripts support fluent reading (P4) |
| Spacing created silent reading | Knox (1968, pp. 432–435); Saenger himself has Ambrose reading silently (p. 8) |
| "The" move | Every parallel case is a bundle (facet F); the candidates in facet H are several |
| Nadella proposes "the exact opposite" | Shared premises and mechanisms (P9, P11 of the post); the difference is authority and seat |
| "Moving work to the artefact" marks the paper off from Nadella | A reference monitor moves work to the artefact too |
| Reading "no longer required the training" | The task was transformed and the scribe took on work (Hutchins 1995, pp. 280–283) |

## Where the argument is exposed

1. **The two tests that separate the paper from Nadella are unsourced.** "What task does the person
   keep" and "whom does the move serve" are inferences `[inf]`. Dekker and Woods (2002) is the place to
   look for support or refutation, and nobody on this project has read it.
2. **P7 to P8 crosses from a medium to a coordination.** External-representation research assumes a
   regularity that holds and a designer who knows the constraints (Hutchins 1995, p. 277; Vicente and
   Rasmussen 1992, V.B). An adaptive, interested mediator offers neither.
3. **P8 collides with the engagement layer.** The best-supported moves work because the participant does
   not read or rewrite the mediator.
4. **Lowering a cost has costs.** Risko and Gilbert name complacency and skill decay; Buçinca et al.
   found the more demanding design worked best `[abs]`.
5. **No experiment tests the contrast in a mediated triad** (facet H, (iii) 1). The paper can propose it;
   it cannot report it.
