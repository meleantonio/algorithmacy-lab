## 2. What can be said about platform intent

**(a) Withholding the rule.** One platform's own words survive: StarWorks' help page says it keeps the score formula private because disclosure "would make it easier for some users to artificially boost their scores" and to "ensure fairness and accuracy" (Rahman, PDF p. 100; page itself unseen). Hardt et al., Kellogg et al. and Bambauer and Zarsky repeat or analyse that reason; none supplies a second platform statement, and Kellogg leans on Rahman. Rahman reads the withholding as deliberate (abstract, PDF p. 5; §3.3; PDF pp. 131, 144), but "strategic opaqueness" is his defined category, and he writes on PDF p. 129 that he has no "further insight into StarWorks' motives". In his own voice the platform's aims are anti-gaming and "maintain a stratified body of freelancers whose ratings were controlled solely by the platform" (PDF p. 128). The anti-gaming reason is therefore the platform's stated reason and Rahman reports it, but it is not the only purpose Rahman attributes; competitive advantage (Rahman p. 47; Cameron and Rahman) and platform interest (Rahman p. 129; Kellogg p. 384; Petre) appear as readings, untested. The ASQ abstract (the only part of Rahman 2021 I could read) gives the platform's aim as differentiating freelancers' scores. Supportable wording: platforms and scholars give anti-gaming as the reason for withholding rules; some analysts read the withholding as serving platform interests; no source tests either.

**(b) Noise and delay.** No source I opened says any platform introduced noise or delay for any purpose. The one measured noise, Uber's jitter, was a bug according to Uber's engineers (Chen, pp. 2, 9, 13; Uber's own account), and it mostly lowered prices. The 5-minute surge clock has no stated reason in Chen. Lee's drivers' "by the second" flicker was reported for 2014 and is unexplained. Bambauer and Zarsky say designers can add randomness and change models often to reduce gaming (p. 14), which shows an anti-gaming motive is conceivable for model noise; they present no instance, and they say nothing about delay. Supportable wording: noise and delay in the signals are documented; the one cause traced was a bug; an anti-gaming design rationale for randomness is analysed in the law literature and not evidenced for any platform.
