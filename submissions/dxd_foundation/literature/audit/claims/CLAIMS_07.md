**Main points for the author.**
1. **Line 417 (table), NYDFS (2015) cited for benchmark manipulation.** The order in the reference list, dated 17 November 2015,
   concerns Last Look and not benchmarks. Its own recitals say the benchmark misconduct was settled in a separate *May 20, 2015*
   consent order (recitals, p. 1). That order is not in the bib.
2. **Line 424, "fined five banks £1.1 billion … for manipulating benchmark rates (FCA, 2014; FSB, 2014)."** The FCA fined the
   banks for "failing to control business practices" in G10 spot FX. Its finding is that ineffective controls let traders
   "attempt[ ] to manipulate" rates. The FSB report records no fines and says its work was independent of the conduct
   investigations (p. 1).
3. **Line 422, "the recitals … state".** The recitals say the bank used Last Look "broadly and indiscriminately" and that there
   was "a lack of transparency … with customers". The rejection of trades that "moved against Barclays" is in ¶13 (and ¶8),
   and the undisclosed reasons are in ¶24, both under "Factual Background". The substance is right but the locator is not.
4. **Line 428.** RFC 8890 does not say that standards adoption is voluntary. RFC 2026 §1.1 does ("voluntary adherence").
   RFC 8890 supports only "end users take little part" (§4.1), and its §4 argues that the IETF should do more for end users.
5. **Line 439.** § 87(2) BetrVG does not itself say that an employer "cannot introduce or modify" a monitoring system without
   agreement. No. 6 reads "Einführung und Anwendung" (introduction and use), and § 87(2) says the committee's award replaces the
   agreement. The prohibition is a doctrinal reading that no card has checked.
6. **Line 436 lists BetrVG among "Recent statutory frameworks".** The Act dates from 1972 (card `de1972betrvg`).
7. **Line 418 calls the Oversight Board "advisory".** Its case decisions are binding and must be implemented within seven days.
   Only its policy recommendations are advisory (Q1 2022 report, p. 12).
8. **Lines 441 and 501, the Carolingian custody claims, have no citation.** Facet O's primary texts support "ruler and church"
   (Charlemagne's letter to Baugulf, *Admonitio generalis* c. 72; both are bib keys without cards). Westwell (2018) narrows
   the claim: for the liturgy he finds no imposition "from above".
9. **Line 485's human-factors maxim and line 487's characterisation of the field have no citation.** The library's
   representation sources (Zhang & Norman 1994; Hutchins 1995; Vicente & Rasmussen 1992) support re-representation. None of
   them says that training or manuals are not the solution, and Vicente & Rasmussen say "No level is globally superior" (p. 14).
10. **Sources that cut against the section.** Casella et al. found that a public, fixed mediator rule raised sincerity but not
    agreement (pp. 22, 24), which bears on Claim 2. Hertwig & Grüne-Yanoff class changes in information representation as
    (short-term) boosts (pp. 977, 979). On that reading DXD's re-representation is a boost and not distinct from both. Niizato
    et al. (2020) computed IIT 3.0 Φ with an approximation on real fish schools, which narrows Barrett et al.'s "not computed on
    any real physical system".
