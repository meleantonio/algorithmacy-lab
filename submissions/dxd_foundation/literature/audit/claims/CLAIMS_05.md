### Upheld (16)

- **C05-36 (contradicted).** Rogers et al. 2021, p. 2: "one must add noise, to prevent differencing attacks". The noise goes into the analytics that LinkedIn returns to marketers, who are transacting parties. Li et al. 2010 §5.2.1: "In this bucket, articles were randomly selected from the article pool" on live Yahoo! traffic. Chen et al. 2015 has no deliberate noise. I found nothing for deliberate *delay*.
- **C05-22 (not_in_source).** Cameron & Rahman (manuscript p. 4): "we know less about how individual workers resist". The boundary statement says "covert resistance enacted at an individual level", and it mentions overt resistance and co-operatives only as other types the study did not examine. Their forum items are tips on individual tactics, for example the RH-Amy-DB advice to cancel rides. That is tactic sharing, not pushing back together. *Fix lead:* the right support is already in the paragraph. Kellogg et al. 2020, pp. 392–393, say that "workers have used online forums to engage in collective mobilization against platforms" and mention "mobilizing of drivers against Uber".
- **C05-10 (overstated).** Lee et al. p. 1607: "The companies communicated only general rules of assignment … and this general understanding helped drivers create their work strategies". Lee p. 1606 also reports that drivers "used their understanding that assignments are based on proximity", a rule the company published. The one observation-based theory in Lee (P23, p. 1606) was a guess that the system had erred, which the authors could not confirm. Weick is not the problem: see C05-11, now overturned.
- **C05-19 (overstated).** Same passage, p. 1607. Kellogg's documentation example (p. 391) concerns Airbnb hosts who "read the company's technical documentation".
- **C05-38 (overstated).** Rahmandad & Gary p. 12: last-training-round performance "for the vast majority of subjects exceeds (p < 0.001)" the random benchmark, and the trajectories show "learning across all conditions". Two further passages cut against the draft. Kahneman & Klein p. 524: "Validity and uncertainty are not incompatible. Some environments are both highly valid and substantially uncertain. Poker and warfare". Rahmandad & Gary p. 14 find a positive delay × uncertainty interaction in study 1 (p = 0.07), "suggesting the intriguing possibility that uncertainty may actually be helpful to performance in the presence of longer delays". Read with that finding, the draft's "combines … noisy, delayed" does not compound the way the draft implies.
- **C05-21 (supported_narrower).** Lee p. 1609: for assignment and surge, the forum discussion went to "social and emotional support, rather than informational support". Drivers posted questions about how dispatch and surge work, but the threads gave little in the way of working theories.
- **C05-30 (supported_narrower).** Rahman p. 89: StarWorks "listed 'inputs' and 'factors'" while withholding "the exact calculation". Rahman p. 117 gives the components as "partially opaque inputs, completely opaque processing". So the criteria were partly disclosed, and the formula was concealed. Rahman p. 35 adds a general claim, "strategic opaqueness", in which organizations deliberately hide "an algorithm's procedures". That supports plurality for procedures, not for criteria.
- **C05-31 (supported_narrower).** One platform's words (p. 89): "artificially boost their scores … to ensure fairness and accuracy". Rahman p. 35 gives a different rationale, "competitive advantage", so the operator side has more than one justification. That cuts against "gaming" as *the* justification.
