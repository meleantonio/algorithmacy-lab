## What it argues
The Guidelines explain what Article 5 of the P2B Regulation requires: "the main parameters that determine ranking and the reasons for the relative importance of those parameters as opposed to other parameters" (pt 2). Their stated purpose is instrumental for the business: the description "aims to improve predictability and help users improve the presentation of their goods and services" (pt 5, repeated pt 15). The diagnosis: "Businesses trading online therefore do not always know the reasons for their performance in ranking or whether and how they could perform better, potentially with the help of paid ranking, despite their ability to reach customers critically depending on this knowledge" (pt 13). Point 94 says users should be able "to make a better determination of whether and if so, how to invest in 'ranking strategies'", including "search engine optimisation and paid ranking".

Limits: providers need not "disclose algorithms or any information that, with reasonable certainty, would result in the deception of consumers or consumer harm through the manipulation of search results" (pt 6, Art. 5(6)); "'relative importance' does not require the exact weighting of the main parameters, or indeed the providers' algorithms, to be disclosed" (pt 46); "an excess of information can mean that, in effect, no meaningful information is provided" (pt 25). Section 4 (pts 76–84): the limits "relate not to providers' commercial interests as such, but rather to potential negative effects on consumers" (pt 78); providers "cannot refuse to disclose ... based on the sole argument that ... the information in question is commercially sensitive" (pt 82); fraud-fighting mechanisms "cannot be revealed in detail without potentially undermining their purpose" but their existence must be stated (pt 73); "a balance needs to be struck between countering manipulative and harmful behaviour on the one hand and the transparency required under Article 5" (pt 84). Descriptions must give "at least a 'second layer' of explanatory information" (pt 22) and be updated when machine learning changes the main parameters (pt 68). Annex I lists more than a hundred illustrative parameters; Annex II lists forms of direct and indirect remuneration.

## Relation to the argument
**The abstract's sentence, in the regulator's own words.** The Guidelines say the point of disclosure is to let businesses decide "how to invest in 'ranking strategies'" (pt 94) because "their ability to reach customers critically depend[s] on this knowledge" (pt 13). That is the author's claim exactly: disclosure is an aid to playing the mediator in order to reach the other party, not a release from it. At the same time the text withholds weights and algorithms (pts 6, 46) in the name of preventing manipulation, so the disclosed text can never be the rule.

**P8 / D3.** A document that mandates a text, limits that text to a "second layer", and warns against too much of it: a literacy intervention bounded on both sides.
