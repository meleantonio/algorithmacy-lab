## What it argues
Customer reviews "enact triadic relationships among the platform operator, buyers, and sellers that generate multiple accountabilities" (opening). Negative feedback was rare (5% of transactions; 72 of 77 sellers above 98% positive), yet sellers felt a "Sword of Damocles" (Online Evaluations and Power Asymmetries). Two asymmetries: at the transactional level, a "gap of visibility" (buyers anonymous, sellers fully visible, sellers "can't give buyers negative evaluations"); at the governance level, automatic sanctions ("When your satisfaction level falls below 95 percent, your account is blocked, without any prior human analysis") and robotic replies (Power Asymmetries at the Governance Level). Buyers and platform form a "coalition of the invisibles" against an "isolation of the visibles" (Discussion).

Of 77 sellers, 41 described "working around the algorithm": contacting buyers directly to have negatives withdrawn, meeting locally, blocking "profile zero" buyers, writing their own terms of trade, and opening disputes so that eBay "eventually kicks him out" (Sellers' Individual Practices vis-à-vis Buyers). Collective action through forums and associations "failed to mobilize, and none lasted for more than a few months" (Sellers' Collective Practices vis-à-vis eBay). The theoretical claim: "practical knowledge of the algorithm may constitute an instrument of power", gained "Through 'practicing' the algorithm" (Practices for Increasing Agency).

## Relation to the argument
**The triad, named.** This is an ASQ paper that calls the platform relation triadic and contrasts it with "traditional dyadic exchanges" (opening). It gives the abstract's dyad-versus-triad move an organisational-theory citation.

**Q3 — coordination cost.** Sellers' extra work is aimed at buyers (to prevent negatives) because the mediator converts buyer feedback into sanctions. The practices are the cost of coordinating with the buyer under the mediator's rule.

**P9.** "Practicing the algorithm" is learning by deciding; the paper says the knowledge gained is practical, not "the formal, abstract comprehension of the algorithmic instructions, which are generally kept secret" (Practices for Increasing Agency). That separates algorithmacy from disclosure in the author's terms.

**P8.** No disclosure manipulation; eBay's thresholds (98% PowerSeller, 95% block) are known and still produce anxiety and workarounds, which supports the sentence in a narrow form.
