## What it argues

Two principles define distributed cognition. First, the unit of analysis: "A cognitive process is delimited by the functional relationships among the elements that participate in it, rather than by the spatial co-location of the elements" (ms. p. 2). Second, the mechanisms: cognition is not only symbol manipulation inside actors (p. 3). Three distributions follow: across members of a group, between internal and external structure, and through time (p. 3). Social organisation "may itself be viewed as a form of cognitive architecture" (p. 4). Culture is "a reservoir of resources" that also "may also blind us to other ways of thinking" (pp. 5–6).

The paper's core principles are stated as a list: people "establish and coordinate different types of structure in their environment"; "it takes effort to maintain coordination"; "people offload cognitive effort to the environment whenever practical"; social organisation improves "cognitive load-balancing" (p. 9). The empirical warrant is the earlier work. The airspeed example matters most here: pilots "use the airspeed indicator dial as a material anchor for a conceptual space of meaningful airspeeds. They only rarely think of the speed as a number" (p. 8), and those features "have been inadvertently removed from the airspeed tapes of all of the current state-or-the-art [sic] cockpits" (p. 7); the newest cockpit restored "a literal copy of the old instrument" (p. 7). The authors redesigned a digital tape that keeps and sharpens the perceptual property (p. 12).

Two further themes. People shift between treating a representation as a stand-in and as a thing in itself, and they "manipulate the properties of a representation to encode information that does not pertain to ... the thing that the representation represents" (p. 14), such as leaving files near the trash can (p. 14). History-enriched digital objects record use (read wear, edit wear, brighter menu items) and show it (pp. 15–16); "Such data, in our view, should belong to users and it should be their decision what is recorded and how it might be shared" (p. 16). The integrated research programme "does not yet exist" (p. 22).

## Relation to the argument

**P5 and D6.** The airspeed tape is the inverse of the scribes' move: a redesign that destroyed a perceptual convention nobody had named, and a repair that put it back. It supports the author's search for "the critical move", because it shows that a single perceptual property can carry the work and can be lost without anyone noticing. The "offload whenever practical" principle gives the abstract's offloading sentence a distributed-cognition pedigree, but it is a principle the paper asserts, not a result it measures.

**D9 (the triad).** The paper's socially distributed systems are cooperative crews that share a goal. Nothing in it treats a third party with an objective of its own, so "social organization as cognitive architecture" (p. 4) supports taking the triad as the unit of analysis without saying anything about a mediator that optimises. The history-enriched object is the closest the paper comes to a log, and its authors assign ownership of that record to the user, which bears on P13 (whose record the audit log is).

**D7.** The claim that pilots "rarely think of the speed as a number" supports the author's "pilots act" picture in one respect, perception over reading, but the pilots are still looking at an instrument, so it does not support "pilots don't spend time reading the situation" (see the existing flag on D7).
