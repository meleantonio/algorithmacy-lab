# Pea, R. D. (1985). Beyond amplification: Using the computer to reorganize mental functioning. *Educational Psychologist, 20*(4), 167–182.

## What it argues

Pea contrasts two ways of thinking about a cognitive technology. On the amplifier view a tool makes the same task faster or stronger. On the reorganizer view, "a primary role for computers is changing the tasks we do by reorganizing our mental functioning, not only by amplifying it" (p. 168). A cognitive technology is "any medium that helps transcend the limitations of the mind" (p. 168); written language and mathematical notation are the earlier cases.

His theory comes from Cole and Griffin's critique of Bruner's "cultural amplifiers," with Vygotsky and Luria behind it. Amplification keeps a signal's structure and raises its strength. The pencil, by contrast, "did not amplify a fixed mental capacity called memory; it restructured the functional system for remembering" (p. 170). Such restructurings "are rarely predictable; they have emergent properties that come to be discovered only through their use" (p. 170).

Three cases show the pattern. A spreadsheet changes what the planner does and when, so planning and hypothesis testing replace recalculation (p. 171). In AlgebraLand the program does the arithmetic and the student chooses operators, so the mental work becomes search through a visible tree of attempts (pp. 172–173). Outliners and Notecards close "the temporal gaps between thought and action" (p. 174). The last third turns to schools, which have "assimilated the computer to its earlier fact-oriented agenda" (p. 175); Pea argues for teaching information-management and decision skills (pp. 176–177) and for building prototypes while studying them (pp. 178–179).

## Relation to the argument

Pea is an independent, earlier statement of the contrast the paper needs for the spacing move: a change in what the person does, against a change in how fast they do it. It supports premise P5 (the scribes' move re-represents the task) and the vocabulary of `ARGUMENT_MAP.md`. AlgebraLand is the nearest match in the source to D7: the machine takes the mechanical step and the person keeps the search.

Two cuts against the author. First, Pea's reorganization "is noncommital" on whether the consequences are good or bad (p. 175), so it gives no ground for sliding from "reorganized" to "ignited literacy" (D1, D10). Second, every case is a tool the user controls and sees. Pea says nothing about an interested third party shaping the tool, so he is no help with P7–P10.
