## What it argues

Wisniewski, Zierer and Hattie re-estimate the effect of feedback on learning from primary studies rather than by averaging meta-analyses. They restate the standard reading of Kluger and DeNisi: "131 studies, over 12,000 participants, with an average effect of 0.38, noting that about a third of the effects were negative" (p. 1). Their own pooled estimate is d = 0.55, with 17% of effects negative, CI 0.48–0.62 and I² = 86.47% (p. 7); after removing 35 extreme values it is d = 0.48, CI 0.44–0.51 (p. 9). Heterogeneity is the main finding: feedback "cannot be understood as a single consistent form of treatment" (abstract, p. 1).

Information content is the strongest moderator. Reinforcement or punishment gave d = 0.24 (k = 39), corrective feedback 0.46 (k = 238), and high-information feedback 0.99 (k = 42) (Table 3, p. 11); "Feedback is more effective the more information it contains" (p. 12). Effects were larger on cognitive and physical outcomes than on motivational and behavioural ones, though the authors warn that few studies measured physical and behavioural outcomes (p. 12); and 21% of motivational effects were negative, 86% of those from uninformative rewards or punishments (p. 12). On timing, the authors note that Hattie and Timperley (2007) found "inconsistent results" for immediate versus delayed feedback (p. 2); timing was not a coded moderator here. Published studies showed funnel-plot asymmetry and dissertations did not (p. 9).

## Relation to the argument

**Premise P10, Convention B.** The draft cites Kluger and DeNisi for the claim that more than a third of feedback interventions reduce performance (line 366). This meta-analysis confirms the reading of Kluger and DeNisi ("about a third") but, in the domain it covers, finds a lower share of negative effects (17%) and a clear moderator: the more information the feedback carries, the better it works. That fits the ordering the draft gives Convention B (valid and plain before fast). It gives no support to speed as such: timing was not tested, and the prior review it cites found timing effects inconsistent.

The result also complicates line 366's second sentence. "Uncurated, rapid feedback" is not what the negative effects in this literature track; uninformative feedback (bare rewards and sanctions) is. Platform ratings and badges are closer to that category than to noise.
