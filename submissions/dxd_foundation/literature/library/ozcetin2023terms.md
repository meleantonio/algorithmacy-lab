## What it argues

Özçetin and Wiltse argue that Terms of Service govern an ongoing, data-producing relation between a user and many actors, and that the clickwrap format is "clearly not fit for purpose" for democratic data governance (§1). They review usability fixes to ToS — nutrition labels, comics, visual policies — and judge the results "notably limited, typically seen in relatively minor increases in engagement and comprehension" (§2.1). The core of ToS, they write, "is not about an interaction between a human and a computer interface, but rather a complex mediation between a person ... and multiple (often very many) other actors" (§2.3).

They make three "posthuman moves" (§4): mapping a service's "ToSsphere" of linked policies and third parties; "decentering" the user by showing the gap between a weather app's cheerful interface and its advertising-driven privacy policy; and "co-performance", which reframes the privacy paradox as "technological gaslighting" and imagines buttons for reporting "inappropriateness" to regulators and other users (§4.3). They gather these into "revealing design" (§5), a practice "aimed at meaningfully making visible these complex networked relations in actionable ways" (Abstract; §7). They want such design to make relations "legible and actionable at the level of interface" (§6) and accept that it will make use "in some ways worse, or at least more complicated or challenging" (§1). They expect it "would need to be mandated at the level of legislation" (§6).

## Relation to the argument

**Ruling D3 and premise P8.** The paper agrees with the draft that text-based ToS fail, and it says so with a review of usability fixes. Its remedy, however, is on the draft's literacy side: revealing design asks the person to see and understand more, deliberately adding friction (§1). That is a re-representation of the rule and its relations — closer to Target 4 made visual than to Convention A. The draft should say plainly that this is a rival answer, not an ally of DXD.

**Premise P12 and ruling D8.** The ToSsphere maps make the third parties and the platform's unilateral power to rewrite terms visible (§4.3: "the companies' unilateral practice of adjustments to appropriateness"). The authors' call for legal mandate (§6) matches the draft's claim that conventions move toward the user only under outside force.
