## What it argues

The review defines intention offloading as "the process of creating a cue in the external environment to trigger a delayed intention" (Introduction) and summarises a decade of work with the circle-dragging paradigm. Its findings: offloading rises with memory load and interruption (Measuring intention offloading); people set reminders more than is optimal, a bias that is stable and larger in those with poorer memory (Optimality); the bias is driven by low confidence even when confidence is unrelated to ability (r = −0.01 in one study), by effort avoidance (financial reward reduced but did not remove it), and by perseveration with whichever strategy was used first (Metacognition; Effort; Strategy perseveration). Children under 9 know they will forget more at higher load but do not act on it; older adults are overconfident and "cannot always be expected to compensate for memory difficulties with external memory aids" (Lifespan sections). Two sentences matter for the costs debate. Fears of "digital dementia" are "overstated because they hypothesise long-term harms based only on short-term evidence" (Strategy perseveration). And reminders work: participants "had a forgetting rate of about 45% when using their own memory but only around 5% using external reminders" (same section). The practical section adds that once offloading is allowed, performance no longer tracks working-memory capacity (Ball et al., 2022), and that people offload high-value items first, so they "can potentially be left with nothing but low-value information in internal memory if the external store fails" (Practical implications).

## Relation to the argument

**Balance in the costs paragraph.** The draft must report costs (`sparrow2011google`, `grinschgl2021consequences`, `risko2019offloading`); this review is the counterweight from the same literature: the harms are short-term and the gains are an order of magnitude. Both sides should appear.

**P5 and "freeing."** The Ball et al. finding, as reported here, is the closest thing in this facet to the author's hope that a design move can free performance from a skill: with reminders available, the correlation between performance and working-memory capacity disappeared. It is a lab result about memory, not reading, and the primary paper was not read.

**D8 (custody).** "If the external store fails" names the dependency a mediator-held store creates, which `risko2019offloading` turns into manipulation.

**Terminology.** The review's "strategic reminder-setting" is the agent's choice, the sense of "strategic offloading" in `gilbert2015strategic`, not the abstract's Li (2017) sense.

## Caution

A review by the group whose paradigm it reviews, and almost all of it is one task family with seconds-long delays. It does not cover strategic settings, counterparts or mediators, and it reports Ball et al. and Dupont et al. in one sentence each.
