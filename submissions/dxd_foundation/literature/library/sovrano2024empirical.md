# Sovrano, F., Lognoul, M., & Bacchelli, A. (2024). An empirical study on compliance with ranking transparency in the software documentation of EU online platforms. In *Proceedings of the 46th International Conference on Software Engineering: Software Engineering in Society* (ICSE-SEIS '24, pp. 46–56). ACM. https://doi.org/10.1145/3639475.3640112

**Identifier:** doi:10.1145/3639475.3640112; read as arXiv:2312.14794v2 (23 Jan 2024) · **Read depth:** full_text (arXiv v2, 11 pp., read whole; ACM pagination 46–56 not compared) · **Source-tier:** proceedings (ICSE-SEIS, peer-reviewed) · **Evidence basis:** direct_read · **Design and sample:** a 17- to 19-question checklist built from P2B Article 5 and the Commission's 2020 Guidelines; manual ratings of the ranking documentation of Amazon, Booking, Tripadvisor, Google, Bing and Yahoo by three Belgian legal experts (one a co-author) on a 1–5 scale; a Prolific study with 134 UK participants rating Tripadvisor and Booking on four questions; two automated assessors compared with the experts (§§4–6) · **Parties modeled:** two parties + third party (business users, consumers, platform), with the regulator as reader · **Facets:** U · **Read:** 2026-10-15

## What it argues
P2B requires platforms to give "the main criteria for ranking offers to consumers, and the reasons for their importance" (§3.1). The paper reports two prior reviews: a 2022 Observatory study for the Commission found that "over half of the business users did not see improvements in the transparency of terms and conditions and ranking practices", and the Commission's September 2023 review found "the basic information provided, for example, on ranking, is potentially insufficient" (§2, quoting the review's p. 9).

Expert averages (Table 2): Bing 3.544, Tripadvisor 2.627, Amazon 2.569, Google 2.088, Booking 1.745, Yahoo 1.228, where 2 means "Indirectly or very poorly answered" and 3 "Partly answered" (§6.1). Documentation was "often dispersed across multiple pages" (§4.1); Google's set had 52 linked documents averaging 1,679.5 words (Table 1). In the lay study, Tripadvisor beat Booking on explaining the mechanism (Q3, effect size 0.42) and its rationale (Q4, 0.44) but lost on guidance for improving rank (Q15, 0.60); only 55 of 134 participants spent more than five minutes reading (§6.2). The best automated tools agreed with experts 63.88% of the time (§6.3).

## Relation to the argument
**Q1 — the state of platform disclosure.** The first independent measurement found of what P2B ranking transparency produced: partial, scattered descriptions that experts rate between "very poorly" and "partly" answered, and that lay readers do not read for long. This supports the abstract's premise that mandated disclosure, on platforms, hands people a text, and P8's claim that the text is not shown to help.

**Cuts against, narrowly.** Booking scored best on "how users can improve the ranking" (Q15), which is the regulation's intended use of disclosure (see `eu2020ranking`, pts 5, 94). So some disclosure does tell sellers how to play; whether that spares them gaming or instructs it is the author's question, not the paper's.

## Caution
Three experts, one of them an author; six platforms; text only (videos excluded); the lay study covers two platforms and four questions with UK participants after Brexit. It measures documentation quality against a legal checklist, not any effect on sellers' behaviour or rank.
