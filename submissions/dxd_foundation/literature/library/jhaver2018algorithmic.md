## What it argues
Hosts "engage in a double negotiation on the platform: They must negotiate efforts not just to attract potential guests but also to appeal to only partially transparent evaluative algorithms" (abstract). Algorithms "are opaque by design, both to protect intellectual property and to prevent users from gaming the system" (Introduction). Hosts sorted factors by perceived control and certainty (Figure 2): reviews, response rate and cancellations were known to count, since Airbnb's guidelines say so; profile edits, instant booking and pricing suggestions were uncertain. The guideline "If you do cancel on a guest, it can negatively impact where your listing appears in search" does not say "how a cancellation might impact a host or after how many cancellations" (Discussion). One host changed his title and description every day "because there's lots of blogs and stuff saying that you need to do it" (P-15).

Coping: trust that "I play the game with you. I don't misbehave" (P-04); reverse-engineering with other accounts, changing one profile element at a time; comparing with neighbours; unique amenities; price. The "paradox of algorithmic transparency": "there needs to be some degree of uncertainty about how these algorithmic systems work or otherwise, some hosts would inevitably game the system", yet "their vagueness creates anxiety among users" (Discussion). Design proposals: alert workers when an action affects their evaluation and tell them when it does not (the daily title change "ha[s] no effects"), share demand patterns, evaluate on controllable factors, mix human review in.

## Relation to the argument
**P8 / D3 — supports the sentence with a mechanism.** Airbnb discloses some factors; hosts still game around what is not disclosed and over-adjust on what is. The paper names the reason disclosure cannot be complete (gaming) from inside the company. That is the abstract's claim in the platform's own words.

**Q3 — coordination cost.** The "double negotiation" is the clearest statement in this round that the strategic work toward the mediator is additional to, and sometimes in conflict with, the work of reaching the guest (declining short bookings versus the acceptance rate).

**P10.** The proposal to detect "wasteful activities (e.g., changing title everyday)" and tell hosts they "have no effects" is a feedback convention of the kind the author's second candidate names: return the result of a decision plainly. It is a proposal, untested.

## Caution
Fifteen interviews, self-report, one platform, with company co-authors; "Nothing in this paper should be construed to discern how Airbnb algorithms actually work" (footnote 1). No behavioural data on whether coping strategies changed rank.
