## What it argues

From the secondary sources only, and from Norman's own co-authored earlier work the library already holds through Zhang and Norman (1994): the book sets out a seven-stage model of action in which a person forms a goal, specifies and executes an action, then perceives, interprets and evaluates the result. The "gulf of execution" is the gap between what a person intends and what the device allows them to do or how it requires them to do it; the "gulf of evaluation" is the gap between the device's state and the person's ability to perceive and interpret it. The terms predate the book: they appear in Hutchins, Hollan and Norman (1985) on direct manipulation and in Norman's 1986 chapter "Cognitive engineering" in Norman and Draper (eds.), *User Centered System Design*, pp. 31–61 (bibliographic details from a course reading list found by web search, not opened). I could not see the book's own wording or page numbers for either gulf. Wikipedia quotes the book (p. 51) as saying of evaluation that "The gulf is small when the system provides information about its state in a form that is easy to get, is easy to interpret, and matches the way the person thinks of the system"; that quotation is unchecked at second hand and must not enter the draft from this card.

## Relation to the argument

**Premises P9 and P10.** §6.1 of the draft maps its two conventions onto the two gulfs: Convention A narrows the gulf of execution, Convention B the gulf of evaluation. The mapping is a reasonable use of the standard definitions. It also stretches them: Norman's gulfs concern a person and a device, while the draft's mediator is a third party with its own objective (ruling D9).
