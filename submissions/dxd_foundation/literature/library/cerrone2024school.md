# Cerrone, C., Hermstrüwer, Y., & Kesten, O. (2024). School choice with consent: An experiment. *The Economic Journal, 134*(661), 1760–1805.

**Identifier:** doi:10.1093/ej/uead120 · **Read depth:** full_text (open-access published version, City Research Online copy of the OUP article, 47 PDF pages; its own pagination runs 1–46, so locators are sections and numbered Results) · **Source-tier:** peer-reviewed article · **Evidence basis:** direct_read · **Design and sample:** online laboratory experiment, 20 periods, groups of five students matched from a ten-person group; non-manipulable market 500 participants (50 matching groups; 12–14 independent observations per treatment), manipulable markets 470 participants (after dropping 140 from groups with time-outs out of 610); Bonn and Max Planck pool, 2020 and 2023 · **Parties modeled:** person–mechanism (a student and an assignment algorithm; other students are the affected third parties) · **Facets:** H · **Read:** 2026-10-13

## What it argues
The deferred acceptance (DA) mechanism is stable and strategy-proof but Pareto-inefficient. Kesten's efficiency-adjusted deferred acceptance mechanism (EADAM) recovers the efficiency loss by asking students to consent to waive priorities that do not affect their own assignment; it is not strategy-proof. The authors give "the first experimental evidence" on EADAM against DA, and note that the Flemish Ministry of Education has taken first steps to implement it (§Intro).

Four treatments: DA; EADAM Consent (students opt in to the waiver); EADAM Object (consent by default, students may object); EADAM Enforced (no way to block the waiver) (§2). Three markets vary whether manipulation can pay and how many rejection cycles occur.

Results in the non-manipulable market (§3.1):
- Result 1: assignments are more efficient under every EADAM variant than under DA; the marginal increase is about twice as large under Enforced as under Consent.
- Result 5: "Truth-telling rates are higher under all variants of EADAM than under the DA treatment": 67.03% against 43.88% (χ² test, p < 0.001). Truth-telling means submitting the true full ranking of all five schools.
- Among EADAM variants, truth-telling is 70.93% under Consent, 68.17% under Object and 62.20% under Enforced (p = 0.004), so enforcing the waiver raises efficiency and lowers truthfulness (§3.1, drivers of truth telling).
- More than half of students consent to the waiver, and setting consent as the default does not raise the rate (§1).
The efficiency and truth-telling results also hold in the two manipulable markets (§3.2; Result 9 is one of them). The authors read the pattern as welfare-driven: students who gain most from EADAM are more likely to be truthful. They also offer a second reading: "EADAM may do so because of its complexity," since a hard-to-manipulate mechanism may leave truth-telling as the default (§1). They conclude that the results "challenge the importance of strategy proofness as a prerequisite for truth telling" and point to a trade-off between efficiency and vulnerability to manipulation (Abstract).

## Relation to the argument
Facet H cites this as showing strategy-proofness is not necessary for truth-telling in the lab. That is right, and the full text shows how far the claim reaches. Only 43.88% of students told the truth under the strategy-proof DA, which fits the line in P8 (Li 2017; Rees-Jones; Hassidim et al.) that a rule which removes a strategic task still leaves many people playing it. This paper adds that a mechanism with no dominant-strategy guarantee did better on truthfulness, so the guarantee of a rule is not what the person acts on. For the paper, this complicates P8's contrast between "rules that remove the task" and "aids that add reading": here a more complex rule outperformed the simple one, and the authors' own explanation is partly complexity. It also gives the author a design relation for D8: the consent right is a convention a mechanism designer can hold and a court can check (the authors cite the Amsterdam Court of Appeals on trading seats after assignment and note the right to judicial review of assignments under Art. 6 ECHR, §1). That is a court-held rule on a mediator, relevant to facet J.

Limits: students have no interests apart from the match, the mechanism has no objective of its own, and the markets have five students and five schools.

Facet check: the facet's one-line quotation of the abstract is accurate; the full text adds the Enforced result, which runs the other way (lower truthfulness).

Compares directly with Klijn et al. 2019, Bó and Hakimov 2020 (`bo2019iterative`, the working-paper version of the iterative-DA result), Hakimov and Raghavan 2020, Afacan et al. 2022 and Cho et al. 2022 on non-strategy-proof mechanisms with higher truth-telling (§1); cites Chen and Sönmez 2006 (`chen2006school`) for the experimental tradition and Hassidim et al. 2017 (`hassidim2017mechanism`) and Rees-Jones 2018 and Rees-Jones and Skowronek 2018 (`reesjones2018suboptimal`, `reesjones2018experimental`) for misreporting under strategy-proof rules (§1). ## Caution
A lab study with student subjects and a stylised five-school market; the non-manipulable and manipulable markets were run in 2020 and 2023 on different samples. Truth-telling is measured on the full ranking, which is strict. The behavioural reasons for truthfulness are inferred, not identified (the authors say their design cannot identify them).
