## What it argues
On why people misreport under strategy-proof rules: "while the strategy of truthtelling is simple, proving that truthtelling is incentivized is not"; the proofs "are often presented as non-trivial exercises to second-year PhD students" (p. 24). The authors review Li's obvious dominance as protection for "a player who can be confused by inappropriately making cross-contingency comparisons" (p. 25). On cue-driven behaviour: Guillen and Hing found truth-telling fell when advice was shown "regardless of whether the presented advice was correct or incorrect"; Guillen and Hakimov (2018) found that advising students to tell the truth helped "but further description of the mechanism reduced the rate of truthtelling (even when paired with advice on the optimal strategy)" (p. 15); such findings "could arise if subjects' truthful preference reporting was not based on their ability to deduce their incentives... but instead on quick inferences about desirable behavior drawn from whatever cues are present" (p. 15). On design: "making strategy-proofness easy to see was a clear design goal even in the earliest stages of the education market design literature" (p. 47); OSP's "potential for application in the context of matching mechanisms is limited" because stable rules and TTC are not OSP-implementable except in special cases, so OSP mechanisms "will be restricted to markets that can be organized by a serial dictatorship" (p. 47). Bó and Hakimov's PAO form is presented as the relaxation with "immediate potential to influence the implementation of allocation rules in common use" (p. 48). "Convergent support for sequential assignment procedures has begun to accumulate from a variety of sources" (Grenet et al. 2022 on information acquisition; loss-aversion models), but "the deep reason for their comparatively favorable elicitation of preferences is not yet fully understood", and similar gains might come "without changing the mechanism—for example, through information interventions" or "explanations of mechanisms that accentuate relevant contingencies", such as menu descriptions (pp. 48–49).

## Relation to the argument
A field-internal review that reaches, in its own terms, the author's fork. It says the dominant-strategy label is not seen by participants; that the remedy of choice (OSP) rarely exists in matching; that sequential, pick-from-a-menu forms help for reasons still unknown; and that the open question is whether re-sequencing the mechanism or re-describing it does the work. That is the abstract's representational question stated from inside market design, which supports looking for a "design change" rather than more disclosure, while leaving open whether the change is to the rule's form or to its description. The cue-driven-behaviour reading (p. 15) also supports ruling D3: describing the mechanism lowered truth-telling in one field setting. Everything reviewed concerns clearinghouses without objectives of their own.
