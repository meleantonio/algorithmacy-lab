## What it argues
Appleton treats language models as "epistemic rubber ducks": "things we can query for fuzzy answers, bounce ideas off, and think through problems with," able to "strengthen our own critical thinking and reasoning abilities in the same way a good debate partner does." She calls the chatbot the interface "everyone and their mother jumps to," says "We are irreversibly anchored to this text-heavy, turn-based interface paradigm," and "it's also the lazy solution." She declines to write the full case and points to three friends' work, one of which is `wattenberger2023chatbots`.

She then describes three sketches from a Figma file. "Daemons" are background characters with distinct personalities (a devil's advocate, an encourager, a synthesiser, an evidence-fetcher) who suggest revisions or ask the writer to defend a claim; "You can always ignore them if you like and the suggestion will fade." "Branches" lets a model propose cause-and-consequence chains for an issue, with the warning "As long as you double-check its suggestions and don't take them as gospel." "Epi" puts moves such as rephrasing, critique, evidence-finding and pointing out assumptions into the right-click menu of a writing environment. No evaluation or user numbers appear.
