## What it argues
The generalized second-price (GSP) auction sells search-ad positions: bids are ranked, and the advertiser in position k pays the bid of the one in k + 1 (p. 2). "Over 98% of [Google's 2004] revenue came from GSP auctions" (p. 2). "Although GSP looks similar to the Vickrey-Clarke-Groves (VCG) mechanism, its properties are very different. In particular, unlike the VCG mechanism, GSP generally does not have an equilibrium in dominant strategies, and truth-telling is not an equilibrium of GSP" (abstract, p. 1). Bidding is continuous and "advertisers can employ automated robots" to respond to others' bids (p. 3). The authors define "locally envy-free" equilibria and show one of them yields VCG payoffs; the generalized English auction has a unique perfect Bayesian equilibrium in continuous strategies that is ex post (p. 3). The conclusion: GSP "developed as a result of evolution of inefficient market institutions, which were gradually replaced by increasingly superior designs" (p. 14) and "VCG is not the mechanism that is used in practice" (fn. 20, p. 13).

## Relation to the argument
The source behind Li's point (2017, §1; 2024, p. 3) that Google advertised its auction as removing the need to strategise when it did not: the paper establishes that truth-telling is not an equilibrium of GSP, so advertisers had to bid strategically, with robots, against a rule that looked like Vickrey's. For Question 4 it is the "before" state of a platform mechanism: the mediator chose a rule under which the strategic work stayed with the users, and the users automated it. Google's later move to a first-price auction (`cox2019simplifying`) did not restore strategy-proofness either; first-price bidding requires shading. For the abstract's sentence this is a caution against assuming a platform's rule aims at relieving the counterparties of strategy; the mediator's rule was chosen for revenue and operational reasons, which is the triad's point. The advertisement text Li quotes is in this working paper too, in §2.3 (p. 7, a section the facet agent did not read): Google's materials "explicitly refer to Vickrey" and say its "unique auction model uses Nobel Prize-winning economic theory to eliminate ... that feeling that you've paid too much" (fn. 8).

## Caution
