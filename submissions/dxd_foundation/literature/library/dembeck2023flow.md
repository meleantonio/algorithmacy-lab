## What it argues
Dembeck starts from Ivan Illich's account that the modern page took shape about 1200: "Der Übergang von der Schreibweise der scriptio continua ... hin zur zweidimensional disponierten Seite ging dabei Hand in Hand mit einer ganzen Reihe von Entwicklungen, die sich Jahrhunderte später als zentral für das moderne Bildungsverständnis erwiesen" (§1, p. 247; footnote 1 also cites Parkes 1992 and Saenger 1997). He says one can feel this by trying to read unspaced text: one starts to murmur, reading is harder, "man gerät in eine Art Tunnel" (p. 247). The new layout lets the reader treat a text as a store to search and move through: it "erzeugt Übersicht und keinen Tunnelblick" (p. 248). For Illich, the move from "monastic" to "scholastic" reading matters more than printing, since setting text on a two-dimensional page is the operation printing needs (pp. 248, with notes citing Illich pp. 121–123).

He then builds two theses on what he calls "biblionome Literalität" (book-bound literacy). First, abstraction (§2): new Latin words in -itas, and "Sprache der Distanz" in the sense of Koch and Oesterreicher, which became a "Leitkompetenz" of modern schooling. Second, independence (§3): once printing makes the new page widely available, the unit that counts is the written "Beitrag" (contribution), and the top skill is to combine many texts into one "eigenständiger" piece in one's own words, which he links to authorship and intellectual property. Two conjectures follow for "the digital" (§4). The difference between written and spoken registers loses weight, and more visual organisation goes with a blurring of the "Sprache der Distanz"; and immediacy replaces the "Beitrag" format with a "netzwerkartige Struktur des Rezeptions- und Produktionsflows," which is "durch algorithmische Rechenarbeit im Hintergrund zumindest mitgesteuert" (p. 253). §5 says that fake-news and critical-media-literacy education take the "Beitrag" as their starting point, and that this "könnte aber sein, dass das an der Sache völlig vorbeigeht" (p. 255); the task may be to teach book-bound and digital literacy as different things. He marks the conjectures as untested: "das muss ja ganz und gar nicht der Fall sein" (§5) and a note that the register shift "wäre im Einzelnen empirisch zu verifizieren" (n. 17).

## Relation to the argument
It is the closest print predecessor for using the scriptio continua-to-page hinge to speak about literacy in a digital setting, and it supports the author's reading that goals inherited from book literacy may not fit the digital (rulings D1, D10: the history as a guide, not a mechanism). It differs in what it draws from the hinge: Dembeck takes the page as the origin of a set of educational goals (abstraction, independence), not as a design move that lowered a cost for learners, and he states no design remedy. His authority is Illich, who treats the page change as a cause of scholasticism, which is a stronger causal claim than the paper makes (D1). The one algorithm mention is a relative clause; the essay does not treat an algorithmic third party.
