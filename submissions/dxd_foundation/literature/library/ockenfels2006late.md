# Ockenfels, A., & Roth, A. E. (2006). Late and multiple bidding in second price Internet auctions: Theory and evidence concerning different rules for ending an auction. *Games and Economic Behavior, 55*(2), 297–320. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geb.2005.02.010

## What it argues
eBay and Amazon run second-price auctions with proxy bidding: a bidder "may submit a reservation price, and this (maximum) price is used to bid for him by proxy" (p. 298). eBay's own advice was to "bid the absolute maximum they are willing to pay for an item right from the start and let the proxy bidding system work for them" (fn. 1, p. 298). Yet many bidders snipe. The authors show late bidding "may be consistent with equilibrium, perfectly rational behavior both in auctions with private values, and auctions with uncertain, dependent values", and "a best response when there are naïve, incremental bidders" (p. 299), but that it is "sensitive to the rules of how the auction ends" (p. 299): Amazon's automatic ten-minute extension "removes the strategic advantage of bidding at the last minute" (p. 299). Table 1 (p. 313): the share of all last bids in the last five minutes was 13% on eBay against 1% on Amazon; in the last minute 8% against 0.1%; by auction, 50% of eBay auctions against 3% of Amazon auctions had their last bid in the final five minutes. Probits (Table 2, p. 315) confirm eBay attracts more late bidding (p < 0.01 in all four comparisons); and experience cuts the other way on the two sites: "more experienced bidders on eBay go later than less experienced bidders, while experience on Amazon has the opposite effect" (p. 315; feedback number positive on eBay, p = 0.008, negative on Amazon, p = 0.059). In a survey of 73 last-minute bidders, 63 had at least once had a bid arrive too late (p. 299).

## Relation to the argument
Question 4's second case, and a sharp one for the abstract. eBay's proxy bidding is a device that lets a person hand the mechanism their maximum once and stop strategising; the platform advertised it in those terms. Experienced users nonetheless learned to snipe, because the hard close made sniping rational against incremental bidders and in common-value settings. Amazon's soft close removed the advantage and its experienced users bid earlier. So the strategic task was removed not by the proxy (the "offloading" device) but by a change to the closing rule, a small convention in the mediator, and the removal showed up as a difference in what experienced users did (13% against 1%). That is evidence for P10 in the author's own sense: a design change in the mediator, not a disclosure, changed behaviour. It also cuts against treating a strategy-proof pricing rule as sufficient: with proxy bidding the pricing is second-price, and the gaming moved to timing. The paper is a field study with a platform that has interests of its own (eBay chose the hard close), which makes it the closest the facet comes to the abstract's triad, though the platform's objective is not analysed.

## Caution
Partial read; the theoretical sections that justify the equilibrium claims were not read. Data from 1999–2000, before "Buy it now" and before Amazon left the auction business. 480 auctions in two categories. Feedback number is a rough proxy for experience (p. 312). The paper does not measure whether proxy bidding reduced strategising relative to a world without it.
