## What it argues
Nielsen names three user-interface paradigms. Batch processing (from about 1945): the user submits a complete workflow and picks up the result later. Command-based interaction (from about 1964, time-sharing; it "has dominated computing ever since"): user and computer take turns, one command at a time. The third, generative AI, is "intent-based outcome specification": "The user tells the computer the desired result but does not specify how this outcome should be accomplished." He says this "completely reverses the locus of control" and doubts "we should even describe this user experience as an 'interaction' because there is no turn-taking or gradual progress."

Three further points. First, current chat tools have "deep-rooted usability problems"; the role of "prompt engineer" reminds him of the query specialists needed before Google, and he adds "don't count on a long-lasting career." Second, the chat interface suffers from "requiring users to write out their problems as prose text," and "Based on recent literacy research, I deem it likely that half the population in rich countries is not articulate enough to get good results from one of the current AI bots" (no citation is given in the article). Third, handing control to the computer has costs: "When users don't know how something was done, it can be harder for them to correct the problem." He expects a hybrid of intent-based and command-based interfaces with GUI elements still in use, and says he is "a GUI bigot."

## Relation to the argument
It is the nearest claim in facet Z about who can use AI interfaces: it makes writing skill the barrier and treats the barrier as a literacy matter. It sets "the locus of control" at the centre, which fits the paper's concern with who controls the mediator's rule, but it stops at one user and a tool. Nielsen offers no convention to remove the barrier and no history of reading or writing. His remedy is better vendor usability ("The same level of usability leapfrogging is needed," as when Google replaced query specialists), which points the same way as the paper's interface-side remedy (rulings D3, D10) but names no mechanism. His claim that the new paradigm "completely reverses the locus of control" is an assertion, not a measured finding.
