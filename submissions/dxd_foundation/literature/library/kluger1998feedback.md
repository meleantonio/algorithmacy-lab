## What it argues

From the opening paragraph only (p. 67). Feedback intervention is "one of the mostly widely applied psychological interventions" (sic, in the OpenAlex text), yet "such interventions yield highly variable effects on performance." In their meta-analysis, "although FIs improve performance on average, they reduce performance in more than one third of the cases (Kluger & DeNisi, 1996; see Fig. 1)." The authors call this "contrary to the common belief that FIs most often improve performance" (p. 67). The paragraph is cut off at "we … found no evidence that information about".

A secondary page reproduces lines I could not check against the article: that feedback effects "have never been consistent or simple," and that under certain conditions "the risk that feedback interventions will debilitate, rather than enhance, performance increases." I record these only as leads.
