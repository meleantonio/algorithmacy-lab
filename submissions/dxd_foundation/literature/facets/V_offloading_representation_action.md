# V — Cognitive offloading, the representational effect, and epistemic action

*Facet V, round 5, 2026-10-15. Agent-drafted (Fable). The author has not reviewed it, and none of the text is the author's.*

**The abstract sentence this facet serves.** "What these settings beg for instead is the kind of cognitive offloading (Risko & Gilbert, 2016) seventh-century Irish scribes achieved when they broke up scriptio continua by adding spaces between words ... exploiting a representational effect (Zhang & Norman, 1994) that enabled readers to internalize text. I argue we should look for a similar 'smoking gun' design change that would free algorithmacy from literacy—enabling people to decide within irreducible triads through epistemic action (Kirsh & Maglio, 1994), strategic offloading (Li, 2017), and ecological feedback immediacy (Shneiderman, 1983; Kahneman & Klein, 2009)."

**What the facet covers.** (1) The core lineage behind those three terms: offloading (Risko and Gilbert and the Gilbert, Risko and Grinschgl experiments that followed), external representation and distributed cognition (Zhang and Norman, Zhang, Hollan, Hutchins and Kirsh, Clark and Chalmers), and epistemic action (Kirsh and Maglio, Kirsh). (2) What the "representational effect" is, its conditions, and which tasks moved work from head to world with what measured result. (3) Whether anyone has applied offloading, external representation or epistemic action to strategic or multi-party decisions: games with changed presentations, shared displays, mechanisms that remove contingent reasoning. (4) The costs and limits: forgetting, illusory knowing, manipulation of the external store, over-reliance, deskilling. Facet E (2026-10-12) already carded Larkin and Simon, Zhang and Norman, Scaife and Rogers, Hutchins (cockpit), Kirsh (space), Kirsh and Maglio and Risko and Gilbert; this facet builds on those cards and does not repeat them.

## (c) Findings by question

### Q1. The core lineage

**Offloading.** Risko and Gilbert's definition requires "the use of physical action to alter the information processing requirements of a task" (`risko2016cognitive`, ms. p. 2); only Box 3 extends it to offloading "'built-in' to the task environment by design" (p. 33). The experiments that followed are now carded. Gilbert (2015) showed people set external reminders more when memory load or interruption rises, that offloading raised accuracy, and that it removed the cost of interruption that appeared when offloading was impossible (`gilbert2015strategic`, Exp. 1a–1b). Gilbert et al. (2020) showed people use reminders beyond the optimum, d > 0.8 in every test, stably across sessions (r = .47), and that individually computed advice removed the bias (`gilbert2020optimal`, ms. pp. 19–28). Grinschgl et al. (2021) showed the trade-off: more offloading, faster copying (49.3 s against 55.1 s) and worse memory for the offloaded layout (5.91 against 7.07 bindings of 12, N = 172) (`grinschgl2021consequences`, Table 1). Weis and Wiese (2019) showed use tracks a tool's actual reliability within a 40-trial block and also tracks a false instructed belief (70.5% against 55.7% of trials) (`weis2019using`, ms. p. 13). Risko et al. (2019) showed the endorsement problem: 100% of participants recalled a word an experimenter had inserted into their file, 77–78% endorsed it as presented, and 45–60% later "recognised" it from memory alone (`risko2019offloading`, ms. pp. 10, 20, 26–29). The group's review calls fears of long-term harm "overstated" and reports forgetting of about 45% unaided against about 5% with reminders (`gilbert2023outsourcing`, Strategy perseveration). Not reached: Dunn and Risko (2016), Risko and Dunn (2015), Gilbert (2024).

**External representation and distributed cognition.** Zhang (1997) is not reachable; Zhang and Patel (2006) report its nine properties of external representations, three of which describe word separation by function: information "directly perceived and used such that little effortful processing is needed", behaviour anchored "without conscious awareness", and a change in "the nature of a task" (`zhang2006distributed`, p. 335). Hollan, Hutchins and Kirsh (2000) state the programme's principles, among them "people offload cognitive effort to the environment whenever practical" (`hollan2000distributed`, ms. p. 9), and give the airspeed-tape case in which a redesign "inadvertently removed" the perceptual property pilots used (ms. p. 7). Clark and Chalmers (1998) license treating the artefact as part of the cognitive process by the parity test and set four conditions on an external store, constancy, easy access, automatic endorsement and past endorsement, by which "The Internet is likely to fail on multiple counts" (`clark1998extended`, §5). Kirsh (2010) adds seven benefits beyond cost, among them re-formulation into "a more explicit encoding" (`kirsh2010thinking`, p. 449), and the dependency cost: "Tools reshape the cost structure of task performance, and people adapt by becoming dependent on those tools" (p. 450). Norman (1991, 1993) and Hutchins (1995, book) remain inaccessible (see facet E).

**Epistemic action.** Nothing new on the 1994 paper; the existing card holds. Kirsh (2010) extends it: for outcomes that depend on "the voting of n participants" or "certain market equilibrium problems", "it is necessary to run the process, and it is best to run the process repeatedly" (`kirsh2010thinking`, p. 453).

### Q2. What the representational effect is, and its conditions

Zhang and Norman define it for "different isomorphic representations of a common formal structure" (`zhang1994representations`, p. 88). The conditions in their experiments: the external rule is enforced by physics or by shared cultural knowledge (p. 106), the designer knows the rule set, and the information is identical by construction. Measured effects in the lineage: solution times differing by "as large as a ratio of one to ten" across Tower-of-Hanoi isomorphs (p. 107); 138 element-considerations against seven inferences in Larkin and Simon's pulley problem (`larkin1987why`, Table 2, p. 77); a speed check turned from "a memory and scale reading task" into "a judgment of spatial adjacency" (`hutchins1995cockpit`, p. 283). Zhang and Patel's category of "cognitive affordance ... provided by cultural conventions" (`zhang2006distributed`, p. 339) names what a word space is, and places the convention, rather than the mark, as what must be held, which bears on P10 and D8. Two cautions. Kirsh (2010) warns that perceptual retrieval is not always cheaper than internal retrieval, especially amid clutter (`kirsh2010thinking`, p. 446). And where an unspaced string has two lawful parses, the space adds information and the change is no longer isomorphic (facet-V note on `zhang1994representations`).

### Q3. Offloading, representation and epistemic action in strategic or multi-party decisions

I found no study that applies cognitive-offloading or epistemic-action vocabulary to a three-party, mediated decision. I found four lines that bear on it.

*Representation of a game, two players, no mediator.* Chou et al. (2009): recasting the two-person guessing game as placing troops on a hill raised dominant-strategy play in a community-college pool from 0% to 46% (65% within 5 of it), with "no changes in incentives, information or subject pool"; the one-sentence hint that discloses the strategic structure moved Caltech subjects from 46% to 87% and college subjects from 0% to 7% (`chou2009control`, Tables 4–5, ms. pp. 5–7, 16). Crosetto and Mantovani (2018): making payoffs less accessible in a twelve-leg centipede game shifted stopping by two-thirds of a node (3.52 against 4.17–4.18), not mediated by risk or trust, while learning slopes were identical across presentations (`crosetto2018representation`, pp. 5–8); the sign of such effects depends on the baseline game (Cox and James found the reverse, p. 9).

*A display shared by many agents and one provider.* Kayongo et al. (2022): in a congestion game, players followed a chart of prior play only two-thirds as often when the chart was public as when it was private (odds 0.67), 25–40% did not follow it even in private, and the "visualization equilibrium" at which the chart predicts the behaviour it induces lay at 0.34–0.35, between Nash (0.22) and the optimum (0.44) (`kayongo2022visualization`, pp. 1, 5–7). This is the nearest experimental triad: the provider's convention is constrained to a fixed point, and the agent's epistemic action (read and respond) moves the thing read.

*A mechanism that removes contingent reasoning.* Martínez-Marquina, Niederle and Vespa (2019): removing uncertainty while keeping every contingency payoff-relevant doubled payoff-maximising play (19.7% to 41.5%, N = 371), accounted for "more than half" of the difficulty of contingent reasoning, and made advice effective (72.5% against 41.5% under certainty; 24.4% against 19.7% under uncertainty) (`martinezmarquina2019failures`, Table 3, ms. pp. 15, 19, 26). The authors tie this to Li's obvious dominance (pp. 30–31): the clock auction in `li2017obviously` works by resolving the lottery over counterparts before the bidder acts. They withheld feedback because, under uncertainty, "feedback is the outcome of a lottery" and less informative (p. 8).

*Multi-mind representation.* Zhang (1998) reports that two minds can be "better than, not different from, or even worse than one mind, depending on how representations were distributed" (abstract only; cooperative task, no mediator).

### Q4. Costs and limits

Every cost the task asked for is now carded with numbers. Forgetting: recall of statements believed saved 0.19–0.22 against 0.29–0.31 believed erased, and folder recalled (0.49) better than content (0.23) (`sparrow2011google`, pp. 777–778). Illusory knowing: self-assessed knowledge in unrelated domains rose after searching, d = 0.35–0.63 across nine experiments, even when the search returned nothing (`fisher2015searching`, pp. 676–682). Manipulation: see Q1 (`risko2019offloading`). Memory trade-off: see Q1 (`grinschgl2021consequences`); the authors add that users learn "how to effectively utilise the offloading device rather than solving the problem at hand" (The trade-off section). Over-reliance: reminder bias d > 0.8, partly metacognitive, partly not (`gilbert2020optimal`, pp. 19–40); a false belief about reliability cut tool use and faded only where feedback was most diagnostic (`weis2019using`, pp. 13–16). Dependency: offloading high-value items first leaves "nothing but low-value information in internal memory if the external store fails" (`gilbert2023outsourcing`, Practical implications); "people adapt by becoming dependent on those tools" (`kirsh2010thinking`, p. 450). Counterweight, from the same group: harms shown are short-term, and reminders cut forgetting from about 45% to about 5% (`gilbert2023outsourcing`). Leads not read in full: Skulmowski (2023) on digital externalisation and manipulation risk; Burnett and Richmond (2025) meta-analysis; Lu et al. (2020) on false recall; Pereira et al. (2021) on perceived reliability of the store.

## (d) What this means for the abstract's sentence

| Claim in the sentence | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The scribes' spacing is "the kind of cognitive offloading (Risko & Gilbert, 2016)" | **Supported in a narrower form** | The definition covers the person's own action; Box 3 covers offloading built in by design (`risko2016cognitive`, pp. 2, 33). Cite Box 3. Hollan et al.'s principle that people offload "whenever practical" is asserted, not measured (`hollan2000distributed`, ms. p. 9). |
| Spacing exploited "a representational effect (Zhang & Norman, 1994)" | **Supported in form; untested for text** | The term fits a change that keeps information and alters access (`zhang1994representations`, p. 88; `zhang2006distributed`, p. 335). No source in this facet studies text. Where a string has two parses, the space adds information and the effect is informational, not representational. |
| ... "that enabled readers to internalize text" | **Cuts against, on the available evidence** | The offloading literature measures the opposite direction: what is offloaded is practised and retained less (`grinschgl2021consequences`, Conclusion; `sparrow2011google`, p. 777). The only passage touching internalisation is Vygotsky, quoted as speculation (`kirsh2010thinking`, p. 451, n. 3). If "internalize" means silent fluent reading rather than retention, the facet has no evidence either way; the draft should say which it means and not cite offloading research for the benefit. |
| A "smoking gun" design change, one critical move | **Supported in a narrow form** | Chou et al.'s Result 4: subjects "either perceive the game form correctly or do not"; presentation changes flipped play rather than nudging it (`chou2009control`, ms. pp. 7–8). Against it, Crosetto and Mantovani: the same kind of change can run either way depending on the baseline (p. 9), and Hollan et al.'s airspeed tape shows the move can be lost without notice (ms. p. 7). |
| "decide within irreducible triads through epistemic action (Kirsh & Maglio, 1994)" | **Supported in a narrower form; the 1994 paper itself cuts against the triad** | Kirsh and Maglio tie the gain to actions whose effects are "totally determinate" with no "other agents" (existing card, p. 543). Kirsh (2010) supplies the bridge: outcomes depending on n participants must be run to be known (p. 453). Kayongo et al. show what running it in a shared display does: the result moves with the others, and following the display fell by a third (pp. 1, 7). The defensible claim is "act to know", not "act and the answer holds". |
| "strategic offloading (Li, 2017)" | **Supported as mechanism; wrong attribution of the phrase** | The mechanism is real: resolving uncertainty before the agent acts carries contingent reasoning for them (`martinezmarquina2019failures`, pp. 15, 30–31; `li2017obviously`). But "strategic offloading" is Gilbert's term for the agent's own reminder-setting (`gilbert2015strategic`), and Li does not use it. Either cite both or rename the Li move (for example, "mechanism-side offloading"). |
| "ecological feedback immediacy" as the third means | **Supported with a condition the abstract omits** | Immediacy calibrates: naive users matched a tool's reliability within a block (`weis2019using`, p. 13), and a delay between decision and feedback kills the learning signal (`gilbert2020optimal`, ms. p. 46). But under uncertainty over a counterpart, prompt feedback is still a draw from a lottery and teaches less (`martinezmarquina2019failures`, p. 8). The feedback must be about the rule, not the realisation. |
| Implicit: mandated disclosure does not spare the person (preceding sentence, D3) | **Supported in a narrow form** | A disclosed hint helped those who already read well (46% to 87%) and not those who did not (0% to 7%), while re-presentation helped the latter (`chou2009control`); advice helped under certainty and not under uncertainty (`martinezmarquina2019failures`, p. 26). Against the strong form of D3: individually computed advice removed the reminder bias (`gilbert2020optimal`, p. 28), so some told information does change behaviour. |
| Costs (required by the task, absent from the sentence) | **Must be reported** | Forgetting, illusory knowing, manipulation of the store, over-reliance, dependency, each with numbers above; and the counterweight that reminders cut forgetting by an order of magnitude. |
