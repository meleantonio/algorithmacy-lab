## Constraints that decide the choice

1. **Generative-AI policy.** The abstract, introduction and outline were drafted with generative AI.
   - The Academy of Management (for *AMR*): "All work submitted to AOM must be created by the authors and
     not the product of artificial intelligence tools unless appropriate to the research question and is
     properly cited", with stage-by-stage disclosure. The text would have to be rewritten by the author.
   - HFES (for *Human Factors*): large language models "solely" for copyediting, with an attestation.
   - SAGE, Springer, Taylor & Francis and Oxford: disclosure of the tool and its use; no ban on drafting
     found. The SAGE wording was read from a mirror and should be re-read on the live page.
   - *JAIS*: no generative-AI policy on its policies page (opened in the browser, 2026-10-13); the page
     refers authors to the AIS Code of Research Conduct, which was not read.
2. **A theory paper with no data.** *Organization Studies* now takes empirical work only; *AMP* excludes
   theory-driven papers; *Human Factors* caps research articles at 4,500 words.
3. **Overlap with the author's other submission.** The algorithmacy paper for *Organization Theory*'s
   special section is due 31 January 2027.

**If "tier A" means FT50 or UT Dallas 24, the only realistic target is *AMR*.** *MIS Quarterly* is the
alternative: the nearest published paper found is there (Grisold, Berente and Seidel 2025), but its
submission rules were not opened.

**If it means ABDC A or A*, the recommendation is *JAIS* first.** It is the highest-rated journal whose
stated categories fit the paper as designed, and the only one of the top three that neither caps the
paper below its natural length nor collides with the author's other work.

## What was not verified

- ABS grades (login-walled; a third-party copy was used) and Scimago quartiles (blocked).
- Scope, article types and word limits for *MIS Quarterly*, *Organization Science*, *ISR*, *JMS*, *IJHCS*,
  *The Information Society* and most Elsevier, SAGE and ACM journals: publisher sites blocked the
  agents. The *Human–Computer Interaction* metrics and the *JAIS* policies page were opened in the
  author's browser and are verified.
- The "closest recent articles" for each journal were found in Crossref and judged by title; none was
  read.
- Time to decision and acceptance rates, apart from *Human–Computer Interaction*.
- Whether Bentley's programme has its own list.

## Before any submission

1. Ask the PhD office or advisor which list defines "A", and whether a second algorithmacy theory paper
   to *Organization Theory* in the same season is wise.
2. Decide whether the manuscript will be rewritten in the author's own words. *AMR* requires it; every
   other journal here requires disclosure.
3. Read three recent theory articles in the chosen journal and map this paper's sections to one of them.
