## The paper

Working title: *The Space Between Decisions: Word Separation as a Guide to Designing for Algorithmacy*.
Current text: `../DRAFT.md` (abstract, introduction, nine-section outline). Sole author, a PhD student at
Bentley University (business school; course context: Foundations in Human Factors).

- **Kind:** conceptual / theory paper. No original data. It proposes a laboratory experiment and does
  not run it.
- **Argument:** human factors solves hard tasks by re-representing the task (the precedent is medieval
  word separation). Disclosure of algorithms (explanations, logs, published rules) is a literacy-based
  remedy and a category error for coordination through an algorithmic third party. The paper names the
  needed capacity "algorithmacy", proposes "Decision Experience Design" with two candidate design
  conventions (strategy-proof presentation; valid, fast feedback), compares courts, dealer FX markets
  and platforms on who can enforce a convention, and specifies an experiment.
- **Literatures it draws on:** distributed cognition and external representation (Zhang and Norman,
  Hutchins, Kirsh), history of reading (Saenger), explainable AI and mandated disclosure, mechanism
  design presentation (obvious strategy-proofness), platform governance and algorithmic management,
  naturalistic decision making, integrated information theory (lightly).
- **Likely length:** 9,000–12,000 words of body when written; could be cut to about 8,000.
- **AI use:** the abstract, introduction and outline were drafted with generative AI (Gemini and Claude)
  and the literature review was run by AI agents with human direction. Each journal's policy on
  generative AI in manuscript preparation therefore matters and must be reported exactly.
