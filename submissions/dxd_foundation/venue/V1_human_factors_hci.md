## 1. Human Factors (SAGE / HFES)

1. **Publisher, ISSN, OA.** SAGE for the Human Factors and Ergonomics Society. ISSN 0018-7208 / 1547-8181 (Crossref). HFES page: "Submitting and publishing are free" unless the author opts for open access after acceptance, which carries a fee; amount not stated (https://www.hfes.org/Publications/How-to-Submit-Your-Work/Human-Factors-Information-for-Authors).
2. **Scope.** The HFES page has no formal scope statement; it describes "original, previously unpublished research" with theoretical and practical value (paraphrase of the page). SAGE's own scope page was blocked: not verified.
3. **Conceptual papers.** Types listed: Research Article (4,500 words, excluding abstract, key points, tables, references; "experimental..., methodological, or theoretical study", not for pilot data); Extended Multi-Phase Study; Review Article (must offer "an original perspective", no word limit given); Replication Study; Special Issue articles. No separate theory, perspective or commentary type. No pre-submission enquiry required; authors may contact the Editor-in-Chief about suitability. A data-free theory paper has no clear home except as a Review Article or a theoretical Research Article, and the latter caps at 4,500 words.
4. **Fit evidence (Crossref 0018-7208).** Dharanikota, Howie, Hope, Wigmore (2024), "Debiasing Judgements Using a Distributed Cognition Approach: A Scoping Review of Technological Strategies", 10.1177/00187208241292897. Gegoff, Tatasciore, Bowden, Loft (2025), "Deciphering Automation Transparency...", 10.1177/00187208251318465. Rieger, Kugler, Manzey, Roesler (2023), "The (Im)perfect Automation Schema: Who Is Trusted More, Automated or Human Decision Support?", 10.1177/00187208231197347. Zou, Rau (2026), "Human-Artificial Intelligence Collaborative Decision-Making in Emergencies: Relative Advantage Theory", 10.1177/00187208261450138. Ben Yaakov, Denisova, Mulugeta, Meyer (2024), "Normative or Confirmative: Effects of Information Quality and Redundancy in Decision Support Systems", 10.1177/00187208241302787. Nearly all are empirical; only Dharanikota (review) is conceptual-adjacent.
5. **Special issue.** None found (search did not index the journal's own pages): not verified.
6. **Review.** Single-anonymized by default, double-anonymized on request. HFES page: average 32 days to first decision; about 15% of submissions accepted.
7. **AI policy** (HFES Submission Policies, https://www.hfes.org/Publications/Submit-Your-Work/HFES-Submission-Policies): "LLMs may be used solely to improve readability and language of the text (the role of a copyeditor)"; "LLMs may not be used to generate content that requires original, creative, analytical or critical thought"; "Authors must disclose in the manuscript whether and how generative AI tools or LLMs were used"; authors "must attest" compliance at submission. IF 3.3 (2025) and SJR 1.479 with Q1 in Applied Psychology, Behavioral Neuroscience and Human Factors and Ergonomics are third-party figures (https://www.journalmetrics.org/journal/human-factors via search snippet; https://www.resurchify.com/impact/details/29755 via search snippet; the pages themselves were blocked or rate-limited).
8. **Fit: poor to workable.** Main obstacle: the AI policy. The abstract, introduction and outline were generated by Gemini and Claude, and HF bars LLMs from generating analytical content and requires an attestation. The paper's word limit (4,500, or an unlimited Review Article) and the empirical culture are the second obstacle. To submit, the author would have to rewrite the generated passages himself, cut to the Review Article frame or 4,500 words, and probably include the experiment's pilot data.

## Ranked shortlist for this cluster

1. **Theoretical Issues in Ergonomics Science.** Its stated purpose is theory and the philosophy of human factors, it runs commissioned reviews and commentaries, and it has published 2024-2026 conceptual and framework papers close to the draft (Hoffman & Brézillon 2026; Gill 2026; Lintern et al. 2024; Minotra & Feigh 2024). The paper's distributed-cognition and representation core is the journal's home turf. Costs: no ABDC listing, third-party JIF 2.0 and JCR Q3, and unverified length and enquiry rules.
2. **Human-Computer Interaction (T&F).** ABDC A (primary); the journal's own (2007) scope names theoretical papers on "the abstract structure and process of human-computer interaction" and design principles, which matches DXD. Costs: expects practical relevance and substantial work; limits and current policy unverified.
3. **Interacting with Computers (OUP).** The only journal where a primary page confirmed a named non-empirical type (Provocation, about 3,000-5,000 words), a long-paper range (7,000-12,000 words), double-anonymised review, a three-week desk-reject screen, and an AI policy that permits use with disclosure. ABDC B. Costs: cap near 5,000 words for a Provocation, and an application-relevance expectation.

Close alternates: Journal of Cognitive Engineering and Decision Making (theory in scope, no stated length cap, but the HFES AI policy may bar the generated drafting) and IJHCS (ABDC B, topical, but empirical). Human Factors is not recommended while the AI-drafted text stands.

**Generative-AI note for all.** The abstract, introduction and outline were drafted by Gemini and Claude. Under the HFES policy (Human Factors, probably JCEDM) that text must be rewritten by the author. Under T&F and OUP/Elsevier terms the use must be disclosed; copyediting is expressly permitted, and I did not find wording on AI-generated draft text beyond disclosure.
