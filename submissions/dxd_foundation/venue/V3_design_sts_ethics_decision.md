## Ranked shortlist: the three best fits in this cluster

**1. Philosophy & Technology (Springer, hybrid).** Strong on genre and subject, weak on business-school rank.
- Its core genre is the conceptual argument, the guidelines state no word limit, and median submission to first decision is 11 days (journal page).
- Klenk (2023), Franke (2024), Fan (2025) and Mitchell (2025) argue over whether transparency is the right remedy, which is this paper's target.
- Cost: not on ABDC (2025 or 2022); the paper must lead with a philosophical claim (the literacy/coordination category error), and the APC is about $3,390.
- Runner-up inside the same logic: *She Ji* (strong fit, no author cost, design-and-economics papers on matching markets, but not ranked anywhere).

**2. The Information Society (Taylor & Francis).** ABDC A, and the nearest topical match among ranked journals.
- Kolkman and van Maanen (2024), "Algorithmic models through a representational lens", and Kempeneers and Simonse (2026) on redesigning disclosure sit on the paper's ground; a reviewer will ask how DXD differs from the first.
- Cost: scope, word limit (search-snippet only: 8,000 to 10,000), review model and OA terms are all unverified because tandfonline.com blocked every request.

**3. Journal of Business Ethics (Springer, hybrid).** ABDC A; conceptual papers accepted up to 12,000 words, which fits the draft as written.
- Cordasco and Véliz (2026) on the right to explanation and Shah et al. (2023) on algorithmic transparency show the topic is live; median first decision 19 days.
- Cost: it left the FT50 in April 2026 (secondary sources), and the paper must lead with an ethics claim (enforceable conventions as a duty of the intermediary).
- Near miss: *Judgment and Decision Making* (ABDC A, a named Theory Article type, no length cap, open access), but its readers are cognitive psychologists and the history, institutions and Φ material would be cut.
