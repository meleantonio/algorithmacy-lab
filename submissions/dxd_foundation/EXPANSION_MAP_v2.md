## Seven arguments the paper asserts and does not make

These cut across sections and matter more than any single thin paragraph.

1. **Why a page can guide a coordination.** The paper says the history is "an analytic guide" and never
   sets out the mapping: reader to transacting party, scribe to interface designer, page to interface,
   finding word boundaries to inferring a counterpart through a mediator, church and court to statute.
   Each pairing holds in one respect and fails in another, and section 3.3 names only one failure. A
   referee will want the mapping stated once, with its breaks.
2. **What "irreducible" means and why these triads are.** Section 2.3 gives one sentence: A's payoff
   depends on B's move and A reaches B only through M. Nothing says what a reducible triad would look
   like, so the claim cannot fail. Integrated information first appears in section 8.
3. **Re-representation against disclosure against training.** The introduction promises to distinguish
   the three. No section does. This is the paper's first contribution.
4. **The cases where disclosure works.** Matsuo et al., Vasconcelos et al., Masuda et al. and the
   explanation result in Jhaver et al. are each mentioned and set aside. The paper needs one place where
   it says what they have in common and why that does not rescue Target 4.
5. **Whether a self-interested mediator can adopt Convention A at all.** Every experiment behind it uses
   a clearinghouse with no objective of its own. Section 2.3 defines the mediator by its independent
   objective.
6. **How Decision Experience Design differs from its neighbors**: nudging and boosting, contestability,
   seamful design, user control, and the four interventions of the author's own published Part 1
   (counter-delegation, structural refusal, bounded operating envelopes), which do not appear in this
   paper.
7. **How a statute about human review sustains an interface convention.** Section 7.3 ends by saying the
   Directive and the Works Constitution Act "provide the institutional framework needed to sustain DXD
   conventions". Neither mandates a representation.

### 3.1 The page before and after — add about 500

- **Now.** Continuous script, Saenger's claim, the Insular change, the limits, his p. 85 sentence.
- **Missing.** The facts that make the history useful to the argument. Three are in hand and absent:
  - **The aid was known and set aside.** Romans used word dividers and gave them up (Heilmann, 2020,
    p. 182). Saenger: the ancient world "did not possess the desire, characteristic of the modern age,
    to make reading easier and swifter" (1997, p. 11). Ancient teachers divided words for pupils
    (Cribiore, 1996, p. 48, snippet). So the convention is a decision about whom the page serves, which
    is the paper's point about platforms.
  - **It came as one of several devices.** The p. 85 sentence names four. The paper speaks of one.
  - **Five centuries separate standard spacing from mass reading.** Standard by the mid-eleventh century
    (Saenger, pp. 176–177); male illiteracy around 80% in England in 1558 (Cressy, 1980, page
    unconfirmed). This is what licenses "guide, not cause".
- **Also missing.** The dispute stated with its positions: Knox, "Ancient books were normally read
  aloud" (1968, p. 435); Vatri's rejection of the physiological mechanism (via Heilmann, p. 181);
  Svenbro's "not an insurmountable obstacle" with his endorsement of Saenger on silent reading; O'Donnell
  (2000) on which part of Saenger is exposed.
- **In hand.** Facets A, B, C, K and `AUDIT_01`, `AUDIT_06`; cards `knox1968silent`,
  `odonnell2000saenger`, `heilmann2020reading`, `tunbridge1992study`.
- **To get.** Saenger (1997) and Parkes (1992) in print. Every Saenger page number wants one check.

### 4.2 The empirical record — add about 600 (priority 3)

### 5.2 Enacted sensemaking and epistemic action — add about 600

- **Now.** Weick, Kirsh and Maglio, a general account of drivers and workers.
- **Missing.** Evidence with sizes that people learn mediators by acting, and its limits.
  - Learning: drivers with more than 2,500 trips earn 14% more an hour than those under 100
    (`cook2021gender`, working paper, observational); `heiland2023social` (trials corrected riders'
    false theories).
  - What can and cannot be learned: stable features are learned; flickering surge and hidden assignment
    are not (`lee2015working`; Chen, Mislove and Wilson, facet M).
  - Doing against watching, the narrowed form of the author's pilot analogy: crews caught 35.4% of
    deviations in primary procedures, 14.4% in checklists and 5.6% in monitoring
    (`dismukes2010checklists`, partial read). Both pilots still monitor (`hutchins1995cockpit`, pp. 283,
    285).
  - The counter-literature and its caveat: experience teaches in markets (List, 2003, abstract), subject
    to attrition (Seru et al., abstract).
  - Formal neighbors: strategic classification (Hardt et al., 2016) and recourse (Ustun et al., 2019),
    facet M and `AUDIT_08`.

### 6.2 Convention A — add about 600 (priority 2)

- **Now.** Strategy-proofness, obvious strategy-proofness, Gonczarowski et al., Masuda et al., a mock-up.
- **Missing.**
  - **The objection.** Matching clearinghouses and laboratory auctions have no objective of their own.
    Can a mediator with a margin run a mechanism in which sincere play is dominant, and would
    participants believe it? The paper should either argue that it can, name the class of platform
    decisions where it can (matching and allocation) and where it cannot (ranking, moderation), or state
    this as the convention's boundary.
  - The experiments with their numbers: `li2017obviously` (36% against 7% of games off the dominant
    outcome; 144 subjects; working paper §5.3); `reesjones2018experimental` (about 23% misreport in a
    strategy-proof match; N = 1,714).
  - Where the removed task goes: `arteaga2022smart` (the warning helped compliers; the task shifts to
    search and stopping).
  - That "showing it is gone" is itself a Target 3 disclosure, so 4.1 and 6.2 have to agree.
- **To get.** Lead, not verified: the literature on credible mechanisms, which asks what an auctioneer
  who can deviate can commit to. Guillén and Hakimov in full (abstract only).

### 6.3 Convention B — add about 600 (priority 2)

- **Now.** The validity-first principle, Larson et al., Kluger and DeNisi, a mock-up, one sentence
  conceding there is no test.
- **Missing.**
  - What "valid" and "noise-filtered" mean operationally, and who vouches for the filter. A contour drawn

  - The cases where fast feedback helped, so the section is not only cautions: real-time shower feedback

### 8.2 The protocol — add about 700 (priority 3)
