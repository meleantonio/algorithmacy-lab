*Alternative title:* *Decision Experience Design, Part 4: The Phygital Context* (kept as subtitle/series marker).

**Form.** Substack essay in the register of the published Part 1 and Part 3: author-date citations (APA 7), headed sections, clear thesis early, case-by-case adjudication, four falsifiable propositions, and explicit limitations at the end. Target length: ~7,500–8,500 words. No undefined jargon; Φ, IIT, and the unpublished hospitality manuscript's constructs remain strictly excluded.

**The route this outline takes.** 
- **Form 3 (Choice architecture) is the baseline:** Most physical environments arrange static menus for single or multiple choosers without reading individuals in real time.
- **Form 1 (Algorithmic intermediary in physical space) is the narrow positive claim:** Holds strictly for ride-hailing curbside matching, and for airport biometric gates where a human commits the outcome.
- **Form 4 (The physical layer as enforcement and exit pricing) is the practical claim:** A ruling becomes a material fact in physical space whether or not an algorithm made it; the decision-experience claim turns specifically on *who is present to answer for it*.

## What the Essay Must Not Claim
- That "phygital" is a settled theoretical concept. Its own reviewers call it vague and practitioner-led (`mele2023phygital`).
- That Part 4 is first to notice computing in rooms, artifacts that regulate, or dark patterns off the screen (`weiser1991computer`; `lessig2006code`; `greenberg2014dark`).
- That the static-versus-adaptive contrast is original. Barlow and Johnson (2026) state it; cite the contrast as theirs (`barlow2026phygital` [abstract]).
- That an electronic shelf label reads the shopper standing in front of it. No primary document shows that (`ftc2025staffperspective`).
- That Robert Moses's Long Island bridges sorted parkway traffic by race. Joerges (1999) thoroughly dismantled this story; do not repeat it (`joerges1999politics`).
- That signage or explanatory disclosure is the remedy. Algorithmacy is not legibility; disclosures show where predictability fails, not where agency is restored.
- Any construct, sentence, or citation from the unpublished hospitality manuscript.

## Section Plan

### 1. Opening: One Decision Point, Two Bodies
**Job.** Contrast two concrete physical scenes to pose the essay's core question.  
**Target Length.** 650–750 words.  
- **1.1 The Kerb:** A driver and rider converge at a curb neither chose. The app committed the match, locked the route, concealed the payout, and started a 60-second timer. Two bodies execute a determination committed by an opaque third party (`cameron2024making`, p. 486; `mohlmann2021algorithmic`, pp. 2007–2010; `rosenblat2016algorithmic`).
- **1.2 The Cafeteria:** A cafeteria line puts fruit before dessert. No one is read; the same layout serves every person; no two-party commitment is made (`thaler2008nudge`, pp. 1–3; `hollands2017tippme`).
- **1.3 The Question:** Which physical arrangements belong to Decision Experience Design (an adaptive intermediary reading both sides and committing an outcome), and which belong to choice architecture? State the four operational marks in plain prose.

### 2. What Phygital Researchers Say
**Job.** Review the phygital marketing literature, concede its self-reported theoretical thinness, and demonstrate its structural blind spot.  
**Target Length.** 950–1,100 words.  
- **2.1 An Undefined Term:** Systematic review shows "phygital" lacks clear academic definitions and constitutes an ontological "mess" (`mele2023phygital`). Disentangle origin lore (2007 workshop paper vs. 2013 agency marketing).
- **2.2 The Seamless Ideal:** The field's obsession with fluid customer journeys (118 papers coupling "phygital" to "seamless"; 1 to "seamful"). Elimination of friction as an economic extraction lever (`johnson2021defining`). The cost of removing friction (`padigar2025friction`; `gustafsson2026unmanned`; `pusceddu2025phygital`).
- **2.3 Problems the Field Identifies:**
  - *Captivity:* Coerced digital participation and elimination of human alternatives (`wilsonnash2026captivity`; `leekim2026coercion`).
  - *Algorithmic Mediation:* Non-negotiable, opaque, digitally enforced rules (`shabnam2026tpsr`).
  - *The Missing Employee:* Neglect of the frontline worker in hybrid service encounters (`mulcahy2026broadening` [abstract]).
- **2.4 What Phygital Research Fails to Model:** No existing phygital framework models an outcome committed for two interacting parties simultaneously.

### 3. The Predecessor: A Room Arranged Once
**Job.** Demonstrate that physical choice architecture already has a fifty-year discipline that explicitly draws the boundary Part 1 requires.  
**Target Length.** 900–1,050 words.  
- **3.1 Atmospherics:** Kotler (1973) introduces space as an intentional marketing instrument to influence buyer affect, operating under the assumption of costless exit (`kotler1973atmospherics`).
- **3.2 The Servicescape's Two Users:** Bitner (1992) recognized that physical environments structure two interacting groups (customers and employees) whose conflicts are settled *once at design time* by the firm (`bitner1992servicescapes`).
- **3.3 The TIPPME Boundary:** Public health and behavioral economists define physical choice architecture as "not designed to be interactive or tailored to specific individuals" (`hollands2017tippme`). This establishes the series' fourth mark (the system holds still).
- **3.4 Correcting Part 1:** Choice architects are frequently unintentional or implicit, not "visible human designers" (`thaler2008nudge`, pp. 3, 10).

### 4. Things That Bind: Scripts, Delegation, and Pre-Digital Devices
**Job.** Evaluate whether pre-digital physical artifacts can function as intermediaries.  
**Target Length.** 850–950 words.  
- **4.1 Prescriptive Artifacts:** Latour’s (1992) hydraulic door-closer, seat belt, and speed bump delegate social and moral obligations to physical objects, but they read no one and hold still (`latour1992missing`).
- **4.2 The Pre-Digital Two-Party Device:** Akrich’s (1987) mechanical electricity meter acts as an arbiter between utility and consumer ("il faut « l'accord » des deux pour le faire tourner"), carrying two marks (intermediary, binds both) but remaining static (`akrich1987comment`).
- **4.3 The Limits of Built Form:** Debunking the Robert Moses low-bridge racial exclusion myth; built form does not bind autonomously without institutional control rights and legal enforcement (`joerges1999politics`).

### 5. Computing in the Room: What HCI Already Knew
**Job.** Reconnect physical DXD with historical Human-Computer Interaction, recovering the critique of seamlessness.  
**Target Length.** 950–1,100 words.  
- **5.1 Seamful Ubicomp:** Mark Weiser’s (1994) call for "seamful systems, with beautiful seams" (`weiser1994invisible`; `weiser1991computer`).
- **5.2 Seams as Affordances:** Matthew Chalmers’s formulation: technical discontinuities (e.g., GPS signal shadows) are resources that users understand and exploit, not defects (`chalmers2003wearable`; `chalmersgalani2004seamful`).
- **5.3 The Seamless Chimera:** Bell and Dourish (2007) demonstrate that seamless ubicomp is an unachievable modernist fantasy (`belldourish2007yesterdays`).
- **5.4 Situated Action & Repair:** Suchman (1985) on the machine's impoverished sensory window and the imperative of providing situated affordances for repair rather than explanatory disclosures (`suchman1985plans`).
- **5.5 Correcting Part 1:** Part 1 conflated seamful design with explanatory disclosure; Chalmers's definition is material and operational.

### 6. The Cases: Applying the Four Marks
**Job.** Apply the four operational marks across fifteen physical and hybrid arrangements.  
**Target Length.** 1,400–1,600 words.  
- **6.1 Ride-Hail Kerbside Matching:** Carries all 4 marks (M1: distinct platform, M2: reads both, M3: binds both, M4: dynamic pricing/dispatch) (`cameron2024making`; `mohlmann2021algorithmic`; `rosenblat2016algorithmic`; `lee2015working`).
- **6.2 The Airport Biometric Gate:** Carries all 4 marks, but reveals that *the algorithm proposes while a human officer commits* (`dhs2025biometricrule`; `gao2022facialtestimony`; `tsa2026pia046e`; `cbp2018pia056`; `adey2004secured`; `kitchin2009airport`). Exit is priced via secondary screening and intimidation.
- **6.3 The Warehouse Rate System:** Functional intermediary between picker and manager; worker bound under termination threat, customer unbound; dynamic rate quotas (`cheon2025fulfillment`; `delfanti2021machinic`; `gutelius2019future`; `california2021ab701`; `newyork2022wwpa`; `conseildetat2025amazon`; `help2024amazon`). Form 4 in its purest operational form.
- **6.4 Hotel Housekeeping Dispatch:** Dynamic room prioritization apps (UpKeep) that shuffle workloads and bind staff while insulating guests (`spektor2023designing`).
- **6.5 Casino Player Tracking:** The house is one party (fails M1); player tracked and bound, house unbound; dynamic deal speed and layout adaptation (`schull2005digital`; `schull2012touchpoint`; `ukgc2026guidancepremises`).
- **6.6 SFpark:** Municipal parking pricing adjusting block by block on a published six-week cycle; demonstrates a *declared operating envelope* in physical space.
- **6.7 App-Required Entry & Cashless Counters:** The system holds still, but exit is priced through structural exclusion (`wilsonnash2026captivity`; `baron2019designing`).
- **6.8 Electronic Shelf Labels:** Regulatory capacity for dynamic individual pricing vs. observed retail reality of static operational tag management (`ftc2024orders`; `ftc2025staffperspective`; `warren2024kroger`; `newyork2025algorithmicpricing`).
- **6.9 Hostile Architecture:** Static, non-sensed physical barriers enforcing exclusionary scripts (`petty2016london`; `rosenberger2023classification`).

### 7. What Physical Decision Experience Design Is, and Is Not
**Job.** Adjudicate the four thesis forms and synthesize the boundary of physical DXD.  
**Target Length.** 750–850 words.  
- **Form 3 Baseline:** Most physical spaces remain choice architecture.
- **Form 1 Narrow Scope:** Physical DXD exists strictly where an algorithmic intermediary reads multiple parties and commits an outcome they must honor with their bodies during the task (`wood2019good`).
- **Form 4 Practical Reality:** Physicality is where rulings harden into facts and exit is priced. What distinguishes algorithmic DXD from traditional physical bureaucracy is whether an accountable human is present to answer for the machine.

### 8. Design Implications
**Job.** Translate Part 1's three principles into physical and built environments.  
**Target Length.** 750–850 words.  
- **8.1 Structural Refusal in Built Space:** Providing genuine, unpenalized, and dignified physical alternatives.
- **8.2 The Declared Operating Envelope:** Pre-announced, bounded adaptation intervals (SFpark) over secret real-time drift.
- **8.3 Conspicuous Commitment over Seamless Extraction:** Why shoppers rejected Amazon "Just Walk Out" invisible billing and demanded the physical "Dash Cart" receipt screen.
- **8.4 The Accountable Human:** Eliminating "human shock absorbers" (powerless clerks) and mandating staff who possess administrative authority to override algorithmic determinations.
- **8.5 The Two Research Gaps:** No empirical study measures algorithmacy in physical settings; no user-side counter-delegation tool exists at physical points of encounter.

### 9. Propositions and Limitations
**Job.** State four refutable empirical propositions and transparently disclose methodological boundaries.  
**Target Length.** 500–600 words.  
- **Four Propositions:**
  1. Real-time adaptive physical systems generate significantly higher subjective entrapment than static physical arrangements with identical exit costs.
  2. Bounded, published physical envelopes (SFpark) yield higher user accuracy than unannounced dynamic adjustments at identical price levels.
  3. The presence of an authorized human override at algorithmic checkpoints significantly reduces unresolved denials of service compared to procedural enforcement staff.
  4. Triadic matches binding both parties at the physical point of delivery occur in only a small minority of commercial hybrid settings.
- **Explicit Limitations:** Batat's framework papers read at abstract level; electronic shelf label surveillance pricing remains largely an unactivated regulatory capacity; corporate proprietary codebases uninspected.
